## Applied

1. **SkillCoach (verified in the full text, arXiv 2607.01874 §4.1, Table 2).** SkillCoach compares its initial and evolved rubrics against human-gold references on 50 paired instances from 10 held-out task families; trajectory-filtering consistency rises from 82.00 to 96.00; its authors call this "a human-gold, judge-assisted audit rather than a new human annotation study". The article had said no system reports calibration for a judge that is itself being edited. Section VII-B now reports the audit and narrows the open problem to calibration maintained across continuing rubric revisions and deployment drift; open problem 3 (Section X) and the S6 claims-audit row say the same. The S6 row also now records RLPR as the one weight-side exception, matching S6.6 and the body.
2. **Letta's local gate.** The released table's only local check for Letta is an optional "Agent reviews before applying" setting; its post documents reflection that "merges back automatically". Section VI now names that check as the basis for the Auto local gate and says that with the option off the system would sit in [None × None]. No count changes (Letta is counted as an industry audit passer, which does not depend on the local gate).
3. **The "nearly every" denominator.** Among the 105 core systems most recently added, 96 have a local loop (the other nine are registries, protocols, and policy layers with no failure-driven loop), and 89 of the 96 decide by program. The abstract, the introduction, Section IV, the method paragraph, and the Table 2 caption now state 89 of 96, so "nearly every" rests on its own numerator and denominator.
4. **The audit population.** The two-loop audit covered 202 of the 204 core systems and 4 of the 10 research systems classified from their papers alone (SkillOpt, FederatedSkill, SkillClaw, ChronoMem); COSPLAY, Cuadros et al., PACE, CORAL, GEPA, Meta-Harness, MOSS, and RCL carry no audit (S4.2.1). Section III and the corpus-flow figure now show exactly that population, so the 14 passers are not presented as a plain subset of the 256.
5. **Appendix XI.** Expectations and the acceptance probability now condition on the history filtration $\mathcal{F}_t$ (the proposal depends on the whole history, not on $\theta_s^t$ alone). The convergence statement is introduced as a conditional statement under strong assumptions that nothing else depends on, (A3) is named as strong, the correspondence paragraph no longer says attribution with non-zero probability suffices, and the claim that a non-stationary deployment tracks a moving optimum is replaced by the statement that tracking needs assumptions on drift and adaptation not supplied here. The one-step inequality is unchanged.
6. **Feedback classes stated as limits, not universal restrictions.** Table 3's C1 cell no longer says "one run per case" (a stochastic agent needs repeated runs); the C4 cell and the prose (Sections V, VIII, X) now say no *immediate* per-edit gate, since a delayed outcome can still evaluate an edit once it arrives; the judged-class human promotion gate is "a reasonable design choice" rather than "a design feature".
7. **Adapters "rare".** The unsupported sentence "In practice they are rare" is gone; the paragraph now says persistent artifacts offer the cheaper path.
8. **Package details.** Figure 1's promotion box pointed to "Fig. 2"; it now points to Fig. 3 (the scope figure in this numbering). CoALA is TMLR 2024 and Lost in the Middle is TACL 2024 in the bibliography. The 48 bibliography entries whose journal line read only "arXiv preprint" now print their arXiv identifier (IEEEtran does not print the `eprint` field), so every arXiv reference carries a stable locator.
