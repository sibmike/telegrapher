## The missing architecture: governed cross-tenant scaffold optimization {#sec:gap}

Four properties define an architecture that no surveyed system instantiates:

- (a) **auto-gated Loop 1**: the commit criterion is an algorithmic threshold;
- (b) **cross-organization promotion**: Loop 2 routes artifacts between organizations (we use *cross-tenant* and *cross-organizational* interchangeably);
- (c) **versioned lineage with access control**: promoted artifacts carry versions, audit trails, and role-based access policies;
- (d) **rollback**: a bad promotion can be reverted without redeploy.

The closest approaches partition the requirements, and the October-2026 sweep sharpened the partition without closing it. FederatedSkill has (a), the strongest partial (b) in the corpus (privacy-preserving aggregation of skill patches across heterogeneous clients that sit under one operator), and partial (c) through an auditable per-modification decision log [@yang2026federatedskill]. EvolveNet has (a), a held-out merge gate at the aggregator, and (d) in the form of a rollback to the previous shared harness after a failed round, with no lineage, no access control, and clients that are benchmark partitions rather than organizations [@nie2026evolvenet]. SkillForge has (a) and (b) within one enterprise with partial (c) [@skillforge2026]. The governance-first registries have (c) without access control and (d) with evidence [@kang2026registry; @qin2026governed; @quessadavial2026acm], and the MCP control planes have access control without evaluation-gated promotion [@agenticcommunity2026mcpgateway]. Rollback now appears inside single deployments, as edit-level reversal [@wei2026skillsmith; @gao2026skillaudit] and as an admission test that rejects edits which cannot be undone [@sah2026evoundo], and inside one federated round; no system rolls back a promotion that has crossed an organization boundary, because none makes such a promotion. Supplementary Material S4 gives the per-system enumeration.

In machine-learning terms, what is missing is a cross-tenant aggregator with an automated evaluation gate at the scaffold layer. It would carry five parts: per-tenant artifact pools, each tagged with provenance; an aggregation step across tenants that respects tenant isolation; a held-out evaluation at the receiving tenant that blocks a promotion whenever it does not lower that tenant's patch loss; versioned lineage with access control; and a rollback envelope on every promoted artifact. The federated-averaging analogy [@mcmahan2017fedavg; @mcmahan2018dpfedavg] is useful only in the limit where artifacts are mergeable update representations; in surveyed practice Loop 2 is selection and merge, and the nearest systems aggregate program edits with a language model, not by averaging.

Whether the architecture *should* be built is a question the feedback-signal class answers (Section REF(sec:signal)). Where the signal is exact, an automatically gated cross-tenant promotion of a test-validated skill is low-risk and the receiving-side evaluation is cheap. Where the signal is an observable outcome, the corner should be filled only with that receiving-side evaluation, because the sending side's outcome does not transfer; Evo2Team's four failed transfers out of twenty-two after target-side confirmation show that even a receiving-side check is necessary rather than sufficient [@wang2026evo2team]. Where the signal is judged, a human gate on Loop 2 is a design feature, and governance means lineage and rollback around a human decision. Where the signal is delayed, property (a) cannot be met and what remains is lineage and rollback without an automatic gate. Naming the four properties converts a vague gap into an audit criterion that any future system can be measured against; naming the signal class says, for a given deployment, which of the four it should expect to meet.
