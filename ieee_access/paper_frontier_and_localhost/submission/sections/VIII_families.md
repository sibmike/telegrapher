## Five research lines arrive at the same loop {#sec:families}

Nobody designed the loop of Section REF(sec:loop). Five research lines, each started from a different problem, built a different part of it, though the parts do not map one to a condition: the candidate (condition 4), the penalty in the gate (condition 5), the trace (condition 3), the gate applied to removal, and the check where a promoted edit arrives (Loop 2). A single research system, SkillOpt, holds all five conditions at once, and the ablation in its follow-up says which of them carry the weight.

### Where each line started, and the part it built

Reflective evolution came from prompt optimization and continual learning: GEPA proposes edits by reflecting over trajectories, and Tiwari et al. argue on two timescales for keeping those edits outside the weights [@agrawal2025gepa; @tiwari2026fastandslow]. Reflective context learning (RCL), the most direct independent formalization of the loop, came from the observation that credit assignment, overfitting, forgetting, and high-variance signals persist whether the learned object lies in parameter space or context space. Its generalization penalty is the complexity term $\Omega$ of the gate [@vassilyev2026rcl]. Starting from orchestration rather than skills, harness optimization works on the trace, which it names as its bottleneck: Meta-Harness keeps a filesystem of candidate harnesses that records which edit helped, and that record is the accumulated history the loop reflects over; roughly two hundred papers cite the line's founding entries, and a dozen are scored here [@lee2026metaharness; @lin2026ahe; @cai2026moss; @zehle2026cantante; @nguyen2026harnessevo; @chen2026harnessforge; @li2026growingharness]. The lifecycle line came from skill pools that bloat under platform limits and built the gate on the way out: retirement is a gated operation in eleven systems, each outcome is attributed to skill, exploration, or environment before an update is admitted, and the complexity penalty becomes a hard frontier once platform limits bind, so the gate trades gain against size rather than weighting it [@zhang2026ratchet; @tanjim2026mocha; @liu2026skillsvote; @zhang2026librarydrift; @wei2026skillsmith; @pu2026skillops; @wang2026assay]. The transfer line came from how far an edit travels and built the check on arrival, from measuring how far one edit's lift carries, one task at a time, to federating skill patches across clients or deployments, with FederatedSkill's server agent applying, adapting, or rejecting each client's patch [@shalev2026ict; @li2026combee; @qu2026coral; @ling2026pace; @yang2026federatedskill; @nie2026evolvenet]. ### One system holds all five conditions

SkillOpt [@yang2026skillopt] treats one markdown skill document as the trainable external state of a frozen agent, and read condition by condition it holds all five: a held-out exact-match split is the measurable loss (condition 1, an exact signal in the terms of Section REF(sec:signal)); rollout and reflection batches are the captured residuals (condition 2); a consensus filter across rollouts is the credit assignment (condition 3); add, delete, and replace edits under a bounded step are the candidate (condition 4); and a held-out gate that accepts only a strictly better score is the gate (condition 5). Across six benchmarks, seven target models, and three harnesses it is best or tied on every cell its authors evaluate. Its transfer across models, harnesses, and benchmarks measures how far one edit's lift carries, which is the evidence radius of Section REF(sec:loop), and governs nothing about how far the edit may go. Its follow-up SkillOpt-Lite [@shen2026skilloptlite] strips the stabilizers (a rejected-edit buffer and a slow update schedule), keeps the held-out gate and the consensus filter, and matches or beats it; so the gate and a consensus form of credit assignment are load-bearing and the rest are stabilizers (Supplementary Material S5).

### What the convergence shows, and what it does not

Two things follow, and one does not. The scaffold-versus-weights decomposition is confirmed independently: GEPA, Meta-Harness, MOSS, RCL, PACE, and the harness-optimization papers all begin from the observation that weights and the external artifact layer are distinct adaptation surfaces with different cost, reversibility, and scope, and a system that updates both keeps them as separate levers [@hebbar2026sia]. No line documents the governed second loop: FederatedSkill and EvolveNet each narrow the gap on different properties, and neither meets the four that Section REF(sec:gap) sets out. What does not follow is that the shape is the best one. The vendors' convergence on one artifact format and one tool layer is the same kind of evidence from the production side (Section REF(sec:design)), reached by groups that were not looking for it; convergence shows only that the same constraints keep producing the same shape.
