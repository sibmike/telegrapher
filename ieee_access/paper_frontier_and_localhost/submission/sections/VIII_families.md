## Five research families converge on the loop {#sec:families}

The loop is not a prescription the field has yet to adopt. Five research families, starting from different substrates and under different names, have arrived independently at parts of it, which we read as evidence that the loop is a shared attractor rather than one group's local optimum. The vendors' convergence on one artifact format and tool attachment (Section REF(sec:findings)) is independent evidence of the same kind. Convergence does not show that the design is optimal, only that the same constraints keep producing the same shape.

The clearest single instance is SkillOpt [@yang2026skillopt]. It treats one markdown skill document as the trainable external state of a frozen agent and edits it with explicit optimizer controls: rollout and reflection batches, add/delete/replace edits, a bounded textual learning rate with cosine decay, a held-out selection gate that accepts only a strictly better score, a rejected-edit buffer kept as negative feedback, and an epoch-wise slow update. Across six benchmarks, seven target models, and three harnesses it is best or tied on every cell its authors evaluate, and it reports transfer across models, harnesses, and benchmarks, which reads as an empirical measurement of evidence radius rather than as a governance mechanism. Its own follow-up, SkillOpt-Lite [@shen2026skilloptlite], removes the buffer, the slow update, and the schedule, keeps the held-out gate and a consensus filter across rollouts, and matches or beats it. The gate and a consensus form of credit assignment are load-bearing; the rest are stabilizers.

The five families instantiate complementary parts of the loop (Table REF(tab:VIII-1)), and each was pushed into its part by the constraint it started from. Reflective evolution started from trajectories and proposes edits by reflecting over them, with a two-timescale argument for keeping those edits outside the weights [@agrawal2025gepa; @tiwari2026fastandslow]. Reflective context learning, the most direct independent formalization, started from the observation that credit assignment, overfitting, forgetting, and high-variance signals persist whether the learned object lies in parameter space or context space; its generalization penalty is the complexity term $\Omega$ of the gate [@vassilyev2026rcl]. Harness optimization started from orchestration rather than skills and names credit assignment as its bottleneck; it is the largest family in the corpus, with roughly two hundred papers citing its founding entries and a dozen scored here [@lee2026metaharness; @lin2026ahe; @cai2026moss; @zehle2026cantante; @nguyen2026harnessevo; @chen2026harnessforge; @li2026growingharness]. The lifecycle family started from artifact pools that bloat and found hygiene to be a precondition for descent [@zhang2026ratchet]: the complexity penalty becomes a constraint frontier once platform limits bind [@tanjim2026mocha], each outcome is attributed to skill, exploration, or environment before an update is admitted [@liu2026skillsvote], and eleven systems make retirement a gated operation [@zhang2026librarydrift; @wei2026skillsmith; @pu2026skillops; @wang2026assay]. The transfer family started from how far an edit travels and instruments Loop 2, from measuring evidence radius one task at a time to federating skill patches across clients or deployments [@shalev2026ict; @li2026combee; @qu2026coral; @ling2026pace; @yang2026federatedskill; @nie2026evolvenet].

Read against the loop, the families support four conclusions. The scaffold-versus-weights decomposition is independently confirmed: GEPA, Meta-Harness, MOSS, RCL, PACE, and the harness-optimization papers all begin from the observation that weights and the external artifact layer are distinct adaptation surfaces with different cost, reversibility, and scope, and a system that updates both keeps them as separate levers [@hebbar2026sia]. Credit assignment is the central engineering bottleneck: AHE, CANTANTE, Meta-Harness, HARNESSEVO, SkillsVote, and the harness-optimizer evaluation of Ong et al. [@ong2026harnesseval] all name the same step as what separates descent from trial-and-error, attributing a system-level failure to one scaffold coordinate, and that step remains unsolved in the general multi-substrate case. Lifecycle hygiene is a precondition for descent, not an alternative to it: a dirty artifact pool starves the gate of signal (Table REF(tab:X-1)), gated retirement is the field's answer, and the one negative result cuts deeper, since once a contaminated skill has descendants, rolling it back recovers little, so gating must happen before commit [@shang2026vag]. And no family has built the governed second loop: FederatedSkill and EvolveNet each narrow the gap on different properties, and neither meets the four that Section REF(sec:gap) sets out.
