## Five research families converge on the loop {#sec:families}

Five research lines, each started from a different problem, have built parts of the same loop without naming it. Prompt optimizers arrived at reflection over failures, a theory of learning in context at the generalization penalty, harness engineers at credit assignment, skill-library maintainers at gated retirement, and federation researchers at the check on the receiving side. None of them set out to build an optimizer for the scaffold. We read that as evidence that the loop is where the constraints lead rather than one group's design. The vendors' convergence on one artifact format and one tool layer is the same kind of evidence from the production side (Section REF(sec:findings)). Convergence shows only that the same constraints keep producing the same shape, not that the shape is the best one.

The clearest single instance is SkillOpt [@yang2026skillopt]. It treats one markdown skill document as the trainable external state of a frozen agent and edits it with explicit optimizer controls: rollout and reflection batches, add/delete/replace edits, a bounded textual learning rate with cosine decay, a held-out selection gate that accepts only a strictly better score, a rejected-edit buffer kept as negative feedback, and an epoch-wise slow update. Across six benchmarks, seven target models, and three harnesses it is best or tied on every cell its authors evaluate. It also reports transfer across models, harnesses, and benchmarks, which reads as an empirical measurement of evidence radius rather than as a governance mechanism. Its own follow-up, SkillOpt-Lite [@shen2026skilloptlite], removes the buffer, the slow update, and the schedule, keeps the held-out gate and a consensus filter across rollouts, and matches or beats it. So the gate and a consensus form of credit assignment are load-bearing; the rest, it turns out, are stabilizers (Supplementary Material S5).

The five families instantiate complementary parts of the loop (Table REF(tab:VIII-1)), and each was pushed into its part by the problem it started from. Reflective evolution (GEPA, and the fast-and-slow argument of Tiwari et al.) started from prompt optimization and continual learning: it proposes edits by reflecting over trajectories and argues on two timescales for keeping those edits outside the weights [@agrawal2025gepa; @tiwari2026fastandslow]. Reflective context learning (RCL), the most direct independent formalization, started from the observation that credit assignment, overfitting, forgetting, and high-variance signals persist whether the learned object lies in parameter space or context space; its generalization penalty is the complexity term $\Omega$ of the gate [@vassilyev2026rcl]. Harness optimization (Meta-Harness, AHE, MOSS, CANTANTE, HARNESSEVO, HarnessForge, Growing Harness) started from orchestration rather than skills and names credit assignment as its bottleneck; Meta-Harness, for instance, keeps a filesystem of candidate harnesses that records which edit helped; that record is the accumulated history the loop reflects over. The family is also the largest in the corpus: roughly two hundred papers cite its founding entries, and a dozen are scored here [@lee2026metaharness; @lin2026ahe; @cai2026moss; @zehle2026cantante; @nguyen2026harnessevo; @chen2026harnessforge; @li2026growingharness]. The lifecycle family (Ratchet, MOCHA, SkillsVote, Library Drift, SkillSmith, ASSAY) started from artifact pools that bloat and found hygiene to be a precondition for descent [@zhang2026ratchet]: the complexity penalty becomes a constraint frontier once platform limits bind [@tanjim2026mocha], each outcome is attributed to skill, exploration, or environment before an update is admitted [@liu2026skillsvote], and eleven systems make retirement a gated operation [@zhang2026librarydrift; @wei2026skillsmith; @pu2026skillops; @wang2026assay]. The transfer family (ICT, Combee, CORAL, PACE, FederatedSkill, EvolveNet, Evo2Team, XRepoSkill) started from how far an edit travels and instruments Loop 2, from measuring evidence radius one task at a time to federating skill patches across clients or deployments; in FederatedSkill, a server agent applies, adapts, or rejects each client's patch and logs the decision [@shalev2026ict; @li2026combee; @qu2026coral; @ling2026pace; @yang2026federatedskill; @nie2026evolvenet].

| Family | Started from | What it built | What it leaves open |
|------------|--------------------|------------------------------|------------------------|
| Reflective evolution | Prompt optimization and continual learning | Proposal by reflection over trajectory evidence; the two-timescale case for editing outside the weights | A persistent artifact; a bounded step; a held-out gate |
| Reflective context | Learning in context as a setting of its own | The learned object in context space; a generalization penalty, the $\Omega$ of the gate | A cross-deployment Loop 2 |
| Harness optimization | Orchestration and harness engineering | Credit assignment over the orchestration graph; source-level edits; slot attribution | A governed Loop 2; access control; rollback |
| Lifecycle and multi-objective | Skill pools that bloat under platform limits | Hygiene as a precondition; a constraint frontier as the shape of $\Omega$; gated retirement | Closing the loop as descent; retiring a promoted artifact's descendants |
| Transfer and federation | How far an edit travels | Evidence radius measured one task at a time; cross-client aggregation; receiving-side confirmation | A cross-organization gate, lineage, and rollback |

Read against the loop, the families support four conclusions.

1. The scaffold-versus-weights decomposition is independently confirmed. GEPA, Meta-Harness, MOSS, RCL, PACE, and the harness-optimization papers all begin from the observation that weights and the external artifact layer are distinct adaptation surfaces with different cost, reversibility, and scope, and a system that updates both keeps them as separate levers [@hebbar2026sia].
2. Credit assignment is the central engineering bottleneck. AHE, CANTANTE, Meta-Harness, HARNESSEVO, SkillsVote, and the harness-optimizer evaluation of Ong et al. [@ong2026harnesseval] all name the same step as what separates descent from trial-and-error: attributing a system-level failure to one scaffold coordinate. That step remains unsolved in the general multi-substrate case.
3. Lifecycle hygiene is a precondition for descent, not an alternative to it. A dirty artifact pool starves the gate of signal (Table REF(tab:X-1)), gated retirement is the field's answer, and the one negative result cuts deeper: once a contaminated skill has descendants, rolling it back recovers little, so gating must happen before commit [@shang2026vag].
4. No family documents the governed second loop. FederatedSkill and EvolveNet each narrow the gap on different properties, and neither meets the four that Section REF(sec:gap) sets out.
