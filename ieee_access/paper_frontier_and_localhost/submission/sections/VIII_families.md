## Five research lines arrive at the same loop {#sec:families}

Nobody designed the loop of Section REF(sec:loop). Five research lines, each started from a different problem, built a different part of it, and none set out to build the whole: prompt optimization arrived at the candidate (condition 4), a theory of learning in context at the penalty in the gate (condition 5), harness engineering at the trace (condition 3), skill-library maintenance at the gate applied to removal, and transfer and federation at Loop 2, how far an edit travels and what checks it where it arrives. Table REF(tab:VIII-1) sets the five side by side with what each leaves open. Read against the five conditions, none of the lines started from the loss or the failure log. The loss each line uses came with the benchmark it inherited, and the failure log was nobody's starting problem. That is why Section REF(sec:findings) finds the loop built from the gate backward: the parts with a research line behind them are built, the trace only one substrate at a time, and the parts without one are not.

Reflective evolution came from prompt optimization and continual learning and proposes edits by reflecting over trajectories, with a two-timescale argument for keeping them outside the weights [@agrawal2025gepa; @tiwari2026fastandslow]. Reflective context learning (RCL) came from asking why learning in context overfits and forgets as learning in weights does, and its generalization penalty is the complexity term $\Omega$ of the gate [@vassilyev2026rcl]. Harness optimization, which names the trace as its bottleneck, grew out of orchestration [@lee2026metaharness; @lin2026ahe; @cai2026moss; @zehle2026cantante; @nguyen2026harnessevo; @chen2026harnessforge; @li2026growingharness]. The lifecycle line started from skill pools that bloat under platform limits and turned the gate around to face removal; once those limits bind, the complexity penalty becomes a hard frontier [@zhang2026ratchet; @tanjim2026mocha; @liu2026skillsvote; @zhang2026librarydrift; @wei2026skillsmith; @pu2026skillops; @wang2026assay]. The transfer line came from asking how far an edit travels; its work runs from measuring one edit's reach a task at a time, through confirming a transferred skill at the receiving team, to federating edits across clients [@shalev2026ict; @li2026combee; @qu2026coral; @ling2026pace; @wang2026evo2team; @yang2026federatedskill; @nie2026evolvenet].

| Research line | Started from | The part of the loop it built | What it leaves open |
|------------|--------------------|------------------------------|------------------------|
| Reflective evolution | Prompt optimization and continual learning | Condition 4, the candidate: proposal by reflection over trajectory evidence; the two-timescale case for editing outside the weights | A persistent artifact; a bounded step; a held-out gate |
| Reflective context | Learning in context as a setting of its own | Condition 5, the penalty: the learned object in context space; a generalization penalty, the $\Omega$ of the gate | A cross-deployment Loop 2 |
| Harness optimization | Orchestration and harness engineering | Condition 3, the trace: attribution of a failure to one slot of the orchestration graph; source-level edits | Attribution across substrates; a governed Loop 2; access control; rollback |
| Lifecycle and multi-objective | Skill pools that bloat under platform limits | The gate on removal: gated retirement; hygiene as a precondition; a hard frontier as the shape of $\Omega$ | Closing the loop as descent; retiring a promoted artifact's descendants |
| Transfer and federation | How far an edit travels | Loop 2, the check on arrival: evidence radius measured one task at a time; cross-client aggregation; receiving-side confirmation | A cross-organization gate, lineage, and rollback |

Two things follow, and one does not. The scaffold-versus-weights decomposition is confirmed independently: GEPA, Meta-Harness, MOSS, RCL, PACE, and the harness-optimization papers all begin from the observation that weights and the external artifact layer are distinct adaptation surfaces with different cost, reversibility, and scope. A system that updates both keeps them as separate levers [@hebbar2026sia]. No line documents the governed second loop; Section REF(sec:gap) scores the two that come closest, FederatedSkill and EvolveNet. What does not follow is that the shape is the best one. The vendors' convergence on one artifact format and one tool layer is the same kind of evidence from the production side (Section REF(sec:design)), reached by groups that were not looking for it; convergence shows only that the same constraints keep producing the same shape.
