## What the field has built {#sec:findings}

Two years of systems, read together, say that the industry has built most of an optimizer one piece at a time and has yet to put the pieces together. The local half of the loop now closes under a program in most research systems and in a growing number of production ones; the newer gates wait for a batch of evidence before they keep an edit; removing a stale skill passes the same check as adding one; memory products ship every entry with its history; the vendors have settled on one artifact format and one tool protocol; and the first federations carry edits between deployments of a single operator. Those six developments are the whole of the first half of the loop of Section REF(sec:loop) and most of the machinery of the second. What is still missing is narrower than the field's vocabulary of evolution suggests: five pieces, all on the promotion side, each absent in a particular feedback class and for a reason that class explains. And the four kinds of system the field has built divide the loop between them, three holding one half and one holding both without governance, which is how an industry comes to have a routine first half and a second half that exists in parts and has never been assembled.

### Six things that changed

The local loop is automated nearly everywhere. Nine in ten of the most recently added core systems close it under an automatic gate, and the pattern holds across substrates (Table REF(tab:VI-1)). Skills are where the research activity is: SAGE and COSPLAY close both halves of the loop inside one research environment [@wang2025sage; @wu2026cosplay]. SkillChain shows the local gate at its most careful, with judge-approved edits re-entering a human reflection gate before they reach the skill bank, a two-stage local gate rather than a promotion loop [@hu2026skillchain]. Orchestration followed the same path, from the automated workflow search of GPTSwarm's optimizable graphs [@zhuge2024gptswarm] and AFlow's tree search over code-represented workflows [@zhang2024aflow] to topologies that evolve under evaluation [@cheng2026optimas; @chu2026agentgrad; @chen2026harnessforge], with TOCOMAS alone keeping a topology change only when it satisfies a structural invariant [@zhao2026tocomas]. The counter-example is the most widespread artifact of all. Instruction files persist across IDEs and agents (Claude Code, Cursor, AGENTS.md [@agentsmd2025], Windsurf, Continue.dev) and appear in a fifth to a quarter of coding-agent projects [@robbes2026agenticmuch; @galster2026harnessconfig], yet in the one controlled study they raise inference cost by about a fifth and buy nothing in task success [@gloaguen2026agentsmd]. A rule file that nobody measures is patchwork at scale, and it is the substrate on which no system closes both loops.

Evidence is batched before an edit is accepted. The earlier systems accepted an edit on one evaluation and absorbed the estimator's noise through the margin alone; the newer ones wait. SAGE-gate commits an edit only when a paired per-item test says its wins are reliable [@wang2026sagegate], and EvolveNet accepts a merge only when it fixes at least as many held-out items as it breaks [@nie2026evolvenet]. In VaG, three heterogeneous critics must pass [@shang2026vag]; AutoSaddler feeds mini-batch failure signals into validation-based selection [@park2026autosaddler]. Two different operations hide under that one name. Requiring agreement among independent proposals drives the false-accept rate down geometrically; averaging over them shrinks the variance only linearly (Appendix REF(app:gates)). Staged evaluation, by contrast, remains rare: AlphaEvolve is the only system that combines a cascade of evaluators with a diversity archive, and the other systems that keep archives use them for lineage, not for evaluation.

Retirement is gated. Eleven skill systems treat removing an artifact as an operation that must pass a check, the same as adding one [@zhang2026librarydrift; @wei2026skillsmith; @pu2026skillops; @wang2026assay]. The reason is in Ratchet's result: skills written by a language model gain nothing over a no-skill baseline until lifecycle hygiene is applied, while human-curated ones show a double-digit gain [@zhang2026ratchet]. A pool nobody prunes starves the gate of signal before the loop can run (Section REF(sec:failure)). Hygiene is not an alternative to descent; it is what makes the gate readable.

Memory ships with its lineage, and correction is not enforced. Letta's git-backed Context Repositories [@letta2026memfs], Mem0's per-memory history [@mem02026history], and Zep's bitemporal facts [@zep2026facts] all record how an entry came to be, so a user who asks what the system remembers and why can be answered. Research memory splits into write-gating and provenance on one side [@wang2026toki; @xu2026ppmf; @margalit2026governed] and verifier-gated failure loops on the other [@yu2026recuris; @wang2026memguard; @sun2026vermem], and only Cuadros et al. does both, which is why it alone reaches the top mark. What lineage does not give is correction: five production backends still return facts that were revoked [@shen2026revoked]. A memory that remembers its own history and cannot forget on request has the ledger of a second loop without its gate.

Formats and registries have converged. Claude Agent Skills, Cursor Rules, GitHub Copilot instructions, Windsurf, Continue.dev, Zed, and Replit all keep a git-tracked markdown artifact attached to a workspace, with an MCP-style tool layer beside it [@galster2026harnessconfig; @barbaste2026harnessengineering]. In the public skill repositories, every substantive edit is human-authored and human-merged [@shen2026whomaintains]. Registries govern what may be used, not whether it works. The MCP registries split into catalogues (namespaces and version records, no scanning) and control planes (scanning, access control, quarantine) [@agenticcommunity2026mcpgateway; @microsoft2026apicentermcp; @truefoundry2026mcpgateway], and the governance registries bring lineage and rollback [@kang2026registry; @quessadavial2026acm; @qin2026governed]; none has evaluation-gated promotion, and none has an inner loop. Tools and governance therefore hold the machinery of a second loop without the first, which is how those substrates reach the top mark with no loop at all (Section REF(sec:method)).

The first federations of deployments exist. EvolveNet evolves a shared harness across data-local clients and merges under a held-out gate [@nie2026evolvenet]; Evo2Team promotes routing skills across teams and confirms them at the target [@wang2026evo2team]; a rule in XRepoSkill survives only if it is verified in at least two repositories [@guo2026xreposkill]; and ShareMem consolidates one user's memories for another, then evaluates them there [@hu2026sharemem]. All four promote inside one operator. AlphaEvolve is the production proof that both gates can be algorithmic at once: an evaluator cascade paired with an evolutionary archive, as FunSearch did before it, with production gains at Google [@alphaevolve2025; @romeraparedes2024funsearch].

No system matures all six substrates at once. Research systems learn fast and lack enterprise governance; production systems govern well and learn slowly; open standards solve artifact portability but not adaptation. The split is not an accident of sampling. Each kind of system was built to satisfy one constraint, fast learning or safe change or portability, and matured only the substrates that constraint touches. That is why the pieces still missing sort by feedback class, and why every cluster of the corpus holds part of the loop and none holds the governed whole.

### Where the second loop should be, five pieces are missing

Walk the second half of the loop from the failure that starts it to the promotion that ends it, and five pieces are missing or rare across the core corpus. Each is missing in a particular feedback-signal class, which is the first sign that the gap is not one gap but several.

- **Failures are not captured** (C2). Deployed memory systems record preferences and successful facts, not residuals, so the loop has nothing to cluster. The one field report of stores written from dead-ends to prevent recurrence describes a single, self-reported deployment [@helwig2026memory]; everywhere else the log that Loop 1 would cluster is never written.
- **Which sessions inform the next edit is left to chance** (C2). Nearly every system takes failures in the order they arrive. Task-CoEvolve's variance-weighted task sampling and AutoSaddler's mini-batch failure signals are the exceptions [@miyai2026taskcoevolve; @park2026autosaddler], and both weight the sessions the loop learns from by how much they disagree.
- **Memory is versioned, but correction is not enforced** (C2, C3). Git-backed and bitemporal memory ship in products, and five production backends still return facts that were revoked [@shen2026revoked]: the ledger exists, the gate that would act on it does not.
- **Promotion is rarely checked where the edit arrives** (C1, C2). ShareMem gives evaluation tasks to a different user, XRepoSkill verifies in at least two repositories, Evo2Team confirms at the target team, and VACE evaluates the candidate with the receiving model held fixed [@qi2026vace]; even Evo2Team's confirmation lets nearly a fifth of its transfers fail on new graphs. EvolveNet has no such check, and the federated optimization framework that proposes a receiving-client gate has not implemented it [@yang2026fao].
- **No promotion crosses an organization** (all classes). The four federations above, and RASO's adaptation of skills retrieved from a public corpus [@chu2026raso], promote across users, clients, teams, or repositories inside one operator [@hu2026sharemem; @nie2026evolvenet; @guo2026xreposkill]. No system promotes across customers or organizations with evidence.

The last piece is the thesis of this article. The four before it are the reasons a system that tried to add it would have little to promote and no way to check what it promoted.

### Four ways the field builds it

Sort the core corpus by which half of the loop it automates and four clusters appear (Table REF(tab:VI-2)). Three of them hold one half of the loop, the fourth holds both halves without governance, and none governs both.

| Cluster | Which half is automated | What the cluster shows |
|------------|------------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Research full-auto | Both, under algorithmic gates | The architecture works end to end and benchmark gains compound; enterprise governance is absent |
| Production hybrid | The local half; a person decides promotion | The production-viable pairing: the reviewer stands in for the exact signal production lacks |
| Governance-first | Promotion only, under evaluation thresholds | Lineage, thresholds, and rollback exist as a product; nothing generates candidates from failures |
| Open standards and registries | Neither; people govern admission and access | The artifact format and the tool layer have converged across vendors; registries admit, they do not evaluate |

Table VI-2. Four clusters of the core corpus, by which half of the loop each automates.

*Research full-auto* systems show that the architecture works when both gates are algorithmic: NanoResearch, AutoAgent, FunSearch and AlphaEvolve, DGM, ADAS, and Voyager [@xu2026nanoresearch; @wang2026autoagent; @romeraparedes2024funsearch; @alphaevolve2025; @zhang2025darwin; @hu2024adas; @wang2023voyager]. DGM is the textbook case: it proposes a modification to its own code, runs it in a sandbox against coding benchmarks, and keeps it only on a pass. The federations described above belong here too. What every member lacks is governance: no access control, no audited promotion, and no gate at a receiving organization.

*Production hybrid* systems show that the production-viable pairing is an algorithmic gate on Loop 1 with a reviewer on Loop 2. SkillForge illustrates it most plainly: a judge that agrees with people more than nine times in ten passes each skill diff, and support engineers decide whether it ships [@skillforge2026]. Sierra routes agent-generated insights through human-authored expert answers [@sierra2025os20], Devin's merged pull requests are its promotion step [@cognition2025devinreview], and Spotify launches automatically optimized prompts and plans only behind an online A/B test [@palumbo2026spotify]. In every member, a person on Loop 2 stands in for the exact signal the cluster lacks.

*Governance-first* systems automate Loop 2 alone. The prompts-as-code platforms [@braintrust2026docs; @vellum2026docs; @langsmith2026hub] and the governance registries [@kang2026registry; @quessadavial2026acm; @qin2026governed] keep immutable versions, evaluation thresholds, and rollback, but they have no Loop 1: engineers author the candidates at a desk, an evaluation blocks a release on regression, and session feedback never triggers either. This is MLOps for scaffolds, distinct from MLOps for weights.

*Open standards and registries* hold the artifact and the tool layer without the loop. The convergence on a git-tracked markdown artifact with an MCP-style tool layer [@galster2026harnessconfig; @barbaste2026harnessengineering] is what Section REF(sec:families) reads as evidence that the architecture is real. Adaptation is what the cluster lacks: its registries govern admission and access but do not evaluate, and in the repositories that run autonomous loops, the runtime state of those loops sits outside version control [@lulla2026loopengineering].
