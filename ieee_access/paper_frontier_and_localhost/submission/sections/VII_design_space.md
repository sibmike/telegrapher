## The four approaches side by side {#sec:design}

The four clusters of Section REF(sec:findings) differ in which half of the loop they automate, and the loop makes exactly two decisions: whether a local edit enters the scaffold, and whether an accepted edit leaves the deployment. Putting those two decisions on two axes gives the grid of Fig. REF(fig:design). Each row is the gate on Loop 1 and each column the gate on Loop 2, with the three gate kinds of Section REF(sec:loop); a cell such as [Auto × Human] holds the systems whose local gate is a program and whose promotion waits for a person. The grid is the comparison the clusters only hint at. It shows which combinations the field has built, which it has not, and whether the empty cells are empty by accident.

```{=latex}
\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\columnwidth]{fig3_design_space_ieee}
\caption{The two-loop design space. Rows give the Loop 1 gate and columns the Loop 2 gate, with None for a step that is absent or runs unchecked; a cell [Loop 1 $\times$ Loop 2] such as [Auto $\times$ Human] pairs an algorithmic local gate with a human promotion gate. Darker cells mark the three populated clusters; lighter cells hold curation, the registries, and the systems whose promotion is ungated or absent.}
\label{fig:design}
\end{figure}
```

One thing jumps out of the grid: the field varies almost only along one axis. Every system with a gated local loop sits in the top row, and what tells them apart is the column, the gate on their promotion: a program, in the research full-auto cluster; a person, in the production hybrids; or nothing, in Letta's Context Repositories, which push every edit to shared memory with lineage and no check, and in the systems that promote nothing at all. Which column a system lands in is not a free design choice. Section REF(sec:signal) predicted it from the signal: a program can gate promotion only on an exact signal or a simulated outcome, so [Auto × Auto] should hold exact-signal and simulated systems and nothing else; a person is the gate where the signal is an observed outcome or a judgment, so [Auto × Human] should hold those and no exact-signal system; and no system should calibrate a judge under edit. All three hold in the corpus. The grid therefore says something the cluster table could not: the industry has converged on the local gate and is sorted on the promotion gate by the feedback it can get, which is the signal rule read off the systems themselves.

The empty cells and the lower rows say the rest. [Human × Auto] and [Human × Human] are empty: no surveyed system puts a person on the local gate of a failure-driven loop and then promotes the result, by program or by person. Only curation sits in the human-gated row, where an agent drafts and a person decides what is kept, as in VerificAgent's fact-checked and frozen memories [@nguyen2025verificagent] and SkillChain's reflection gate. The bottom row holds the systems with no failure-driven loop at all, which enter the grid as contrast entries (Supplementary Material S4). In [None × Auto] sit the governance-first platforms, which gate promotion on evaluation but generate no candidates; in [None × Human], the registries and community hubs in which a person registers an artifact and a person, or an admission scan, approves its use [@agenticcommunity2026mcpgateway; @microsoft2026apicentermcp; @truefoundry2026mcpgateway; @jiang2026buildrix]. One field study finds the same shape in practice: in its five public skill repositories every substantive edit is human-authored and human-merged, with one deprecation in 254 edits [@shen2026whomaintains]. So the bottom row is where the governance machinery lives and the top row is where the learning lives, and no system spans both under a gate on both axes.

Adding the seven systems that promote across users, teams, or repositories with no versioning or lineage would not change the picture; they would join cells already occupied, and none would open a new one (Supplementary Material S2). What no cell contains is governed cross-tenant promotion, and Section REF(sec:gap) says what it would take.
