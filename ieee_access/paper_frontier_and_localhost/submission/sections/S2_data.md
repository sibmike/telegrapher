# S2. Data appendix


**Corpus and window.** The corpus holds 256 unique systems from January 2023 to the October 2026 cutoff, released as two files. `p3_master_scores.csv` holds the 130 first-pass systems in 140 rows; `p3_refresh_2026-10.csv` holds the 126 second-pass systems in 133 rows, four of which re-audit a first-pass system and replace its row rather than add a system. The corpus therefore has 140 + 133 − 4 = 269 substrate-level rows. A system may be scored on several substrates, so rows outnumber systems, and Table V-2 counts rows.

**Two passes.** Every system in either pass is scored against one rubric, from its own method section or documentation. First-pass systems were found in arXiv, vendor documentation, and public repositories through May 2026. Second-pass systems, found from June to the October 2026 cutoff, come from six query families: self-evolving skills and skill lifecycle; harness and context optimization; agent memory governance; prompt-as-code and evaluation-gated promotion; federated or multi-tenant skill sharing; and multi-agent topology evolution. Some were reached instead through the citations of the first-pass systems closest to the missing architecture of Section IX. The second pass was a systematic search of the field, not only a search around the known systems, so the per-substrate statistics pool both passes.

**Inclusion and exclusion.** A system enters the corpus if it either wraps a foundation model with at least one persistent artifact updated from deployment signal or is described in public as "self-improving," "scaffolded," or "agentic." The contrast set enters through the second criterion and is kept on purpose, so that the discriminator has something to mark against (Self-Refine, ReAct, TextGrad, OPRO, engineer-authored convention files; Section S4.1.1). Two kinds of system are excluded: those whose only update mechanism is weight-level fine-tuning or reinforcement learning, which are model-side and belong to the self-evolution literature this survey positions against, and those with no public evidence of deployment or release, such as white papers without code, demos, or a production claim.

**Two filters.** The patch-local discriminator admits a system to the core corpus when it scores at least 3 on at least one substrate. Of the 256 systems, 204 pass it, 99 of them first-pass. The other 52, 31 of them first-pass, form the contrast corpus: systems described in public as agentic or self-improving that fail the discriminator. Each second-pass contrast row gives its exclusion reason in the released CSV. The stricter composite two-loop audit then asks whether Loop 1 modifies a persisted artifact from session signal and whether Loop 2 has an explicit cross-context promotion mechanism with versioning or lineage. Representative first-pass members of both groups, contrast systems and core systems that fail the composite audit, are listed in Section S4.1.

**Systems outside the released table.** Fifteen named systems have no row in the released table. Ten are treated at mechanism level in the main text and this supplement: SkillOpt, GEPA, Meta-Harness, CORAL, PACE, MOSS, RCL, FederatedSkill, SkillClaw, and ChronoMem. Each was classified from its paper after the first pass closed and is placed by that reading, in the matrix or among the research families. The other five are contrast or boundary entries, classified from their papers and marked with a dagger in Section S4.1: ReAct, MetaGen, MetaReflection, CLIN, and ACE. The corpus counts (256, 204, 52) exclude all fifteen. SkillOpt is nonetheless one of the fourteen research systems that pass the composite audit, because the audit was applied to it from its paper.

**Evidence tiers.** Each system carries one or more of five tiers: (T1) peer-reviewed publication; (T2) arXiv preprint; (T3) production claim with metrics; (T4) open-source artifact; (T5) industry blog or interview. T1 and T4 weigh most in cluster analysis. T3 counts where the claim is concrete, naming a benchmark or an integration target; T5 counts only when T1–T4 evidence corroborates it elsewhere. Tier flags reach cluster claims through the per-row evidence column of the released CSV, and T3 production claims are never pooled with T1 or T2 evidence in a cluster-level statistic.

**Tier distribution.** In the first-pass core corpus, about a quarter of the systems carry T1 evidence as their primary tier, roughly half carry T2, and the rest T3 or T4. Preprint-only systems in either pass carry T2, recorded per row in the released CSV. Some T2 work is recent enough that its ablations may yet be revised; the cluster-level claims survive dropping any single T2 system.

**Scoring discipline.** Each system is scored substrate by substrate on the survey's 0–5 rubric and also receives an integrated patch-local-adaptation score in {0, 1, 2, 2.5, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5, 5}. Half-scores are used sparingly, where one criterion is clearly met and another only partly, and ambiguous cases are rounded down. Every score comes from a single rater. The full score table is therefore published, each row with a one-line justification in the released CSVs, so that any ambiguous classification can be checked independently (Section S1).

**Composite two-loop criterion (sensitivity analysis).** The strict composite audit passes fourteen research systems, all first-pass except SkillOpt, and three industry exemplars. Two natural relaxations change that count.

The governance relaxation counts any of versioning, lineage, or rollback as Loop-2 governance. It re-admits MAE, MetaGen, MetaReflection, and CLIN from the first pass, raising the research count to approximately eighteen, and of the second-pass promoters below it admits EvolveNet alone, whose rollback to the previous shared harness counts.

Seven second-pass systems promote across users, clients, teams, or repositories with no versioning or lineage on the promoted artifact: EvolveNet, Evo2Team, XRepoSkill, ShareMem, RASO, Spotify's recommendation loop, and Future AGI's prompt lifecycle. They fail the audit on governance alone. A weaker relaxation that asks only for an explicit promotion mechanism, with no governance property at all, would admit six of them, all landing in [Auto × Auto] except Spotify's loop, which would land in [Auto × Human]; none would open a new cell. The seventh, Future AGI's prompt lifecycle, rests on T5 evidence alone, which the tier rule above does not count without corroboration, so it is not admitted under any rule.

The optimizer-state relaxation admits as Loop 1 a session signal that updates optimizer state without persisting an artifact distinct from that state. It re-admits DSPy and TextGrad from the first pass, raising the research count to approximately sixteen. It admits no second-pass system. The second-pass contrast rows are benchmarks, evaluation and attack studies, registries, audits, and conceptual frameworks, plus one orchestrator whose adaptation lives in the weights, and none of them persists optimizer state as its only artifact.

The qualitative findings hold under every relaxation: [Auto × Auto] is well populated; [Human × Auto] and [Human × Human] are empty; [Human × None] holds only curation workflows and [None × Human] only registries, all of which fail the composite audit; the governance-first cluster sits in [None × Auto]; Letta alone passes the audit with an ungated promotion; and no system adds governed cross-tenant promotion.
