# Ledger part J4 — How models use context, and what extra tokens cost

Audited 2026-10-03. Every card below was written from the full text, downloaded and searched locally (pdftotext plus a phrase-to-page script), not from a fetch summary. Sources read: ACL Anthology PDFs and .bib exports (Khandelwal 2018, Sun 2021, Levy 2024); the PMLR v202 page, PDF and BibTeX (Shi 2023); the ICLR 2025 proceedings page, PDF and BibTeX (GSM-Symbolic); the NeurIPS 2022 proceedings page, PDF, Supplemental PDF and BibTeX (Wei 2022); the Transformer Circuits HTML article and its suggested BibTeX (Olsson 2022); the TMLR accepted-papers list at jmlr.org/tmlr/papers and its BibTeX export (Yang et al.); the COLM 2024 accepted-papers page (RULER); and the arXiv PDFs of all ten works (latest version, plus v1 for RULER and Yang et al. to trace changes). Values that exist only in figures were read from page renders at 200–600 dpi (Kaplan Fig. 20 legend; GSM-Symbolic Fig. 8a and Table 1; Shi Table 3; RULER Table 3; Wei Fig. 2). Each arXiv abs page was also checked for a withdrawal notice. ### khandelwal2018sharp
- Source of record: https://aclanthology.org/P18-1027/ (ACL 2018, Proceedings of the 56th Annual Meeting of the ACL, Volume 1: Long Papers, pp. 284–294, DOI 10.18653/v1/P18-1027). Access: full text (https://aclanthology.org/P18-1027.pdf; arXiv v1 https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.04623v1, which adds Appendices A–B; the passages used are identical).
- Retraction: none found. The Crossref record 10.18653/v1/P18-1027 has no relation, update-to or updated-by field, and `filter=updates` returns 0 notices. The "Retraction" query found nothing. The arXiv abs page (v1 only) has no withdrawal notice.
- Card:
  - Setting: One architecture, a standard 3-layer AWD-LSTM (400-d embeddings, 1150 hidden units, the default settings of Merity et al. 2018; arXiv App. A, Table 2). It was trained normally, and its context was perturbed only at test time (truncated, shuffled, reversed, replaced, or with words dropped). Data were Penn Treebank (0.9M training tokens, 10K vocabulary) and WikiText-2 (2.1M, 33K vocabulary). Results are dev-set loss and perplexity, averaged over three seeds, under teacher forcing (Sec. 3, pp. 285–286).
  - What it establishes:
    - The effective context, defined as the context length whose loss is within about 1–2% perplexity of the full history, is about 200 tokens on average. Past 150 tokens (PTB) or 250 tokens (WikiText-2), perplexity changes by only about 1% (Sec. 4, p. 286; Fig. 1a caption, p. 287). This holds across hyperparameter variants (Fig. 1b). Frequent words are insensitive to context beyond 50 tokens, while infrequent words need more than 200 (Sec. 4.1, p. 286).
    - Local order, tested by permuting 20-token spans, matters only within the most recent 20 tokens. Global order, tested by shuffling or reversing everything beyond a point, has no effect beyond 50 tokens. Replacing those distant words with random training text does raise loss, so the identity of distant words still matters but their order does not (Sec. 5.1 and Discussion, Fig. 2, p. 288; the PTB counterpart is arXiv Fig. 8). The authors then fix 50 tokens as the boundary between nearby and long-range context.
    - Words that can only be copied from beyond 50 tokens depend more on the coarse distant context than on the earlier occurrence itself: dropping all distant context costs 12% perplexity, while dropping only the target's earlier occurrences costs 2%. The neural cache mainly helps copy such words (Sec. 6, pp. 290–291).
  - Headline numbers (verbatim, with location): "capable of using about 200 tokens of context on average" (Abstract, p. 284). Printed values: effective context 150 (PTB) and 250 (Wiki) (Fig. 1a caption, p. 287); global order irrelevant beyond 50 tokens (Fig. 2b caption, p. 288).
  - Authors' own stated limitations or scope: model and data are tightly coupled, and confounds such as vocabulary and dataset size make it hard to separate model behaviour from properties of the language. Only a standard LSTM was studied, and other model classes and languages are left open (Sec. 7, p. 292). The analysis is token-level only (Sec. 8, p. 292). Because perturbations are applied only at test time, the measured losses are upper bounds (Sec. 3, p. 285).
  - Relation to the review: RQ1 (importance), as a precedent. On the input side, only a short recent window is used in an order-sensitive way. The distant past contributes coarse, order-free content, and copying from far away leans on an external cache. Context positions are not used uniformly.
  - The hinge: the study measures teacher-forced perplexity of a 2018 LSTM on news and Wikipedia text, with the input context perturbed. The review asks where errors arise inside long answers generated by current Transformer LLMs.
- Use 1 — intended claim: "LSTM language models make use of a few hundred tokens of context on average, but word order matters only within roughly the most recent fifty tokens; more distant context acts as a rough topic signal."
  - Source passage: "capable of using about 200 tokens of context on average" (Abstract, p. 284). The 50-token result is in Sec. 5.1 and Fig. 2b (p. 288). Reading the distant past as a rough semantic field or topic is the authors' interpretation, which they present as suggestive rather than shown (Abstract, p. 284; Sec. 5.1 Discussion, p. 288).
  - Scope: one LSTM architecture with hyperparameter variants, two small corpora, dev-set perplexity, and test-time perturbations only.
  - Verdict: NARROW. "A few hundred" overstates the reported value, which is about 200 (150 and 250). "LSTM language models" generalises from one architecture. The topic reading is an interpretation, and distant word identity still matters. Safe wording: "An LSTM language model (AWD-LSTM, tested on Penn Treebank and WikiText-2) used about 200 tokens of context on average: cutting its context to the most recent 150 tokens (Penn Treebank) or 250 tokens (WikiText-2) raised perplexity by only about 1%. Shuffling or reversing the words more than 50 tokens back left its loss unchanged, whereas replacing them with other text raised it; the authors read this as the distant past being kept only as a rough semantic or topical representation [khandelwal2018sharp]."
- Bib notes: taken from the ACL Anthology .bib (P18-1027). Authors, title, booktitle, pages 284–294, address, publisher and DOI agree with Crossref. Editors and month are omitted for IEEE style.

### sun2021longrange
- Source of record: https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.62/ (EMNLP 2021, Proceedings of the 2021 Conference on Empirical Methods in Natural Language Processing, pp. 807–822, DOI 10.18653/v1/2021.emnlp-main.62). Access: full text (https://aclanthology.org/2021.emnlp-main.62.pdf, including Appendices A–F; arXiv v1 https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.09115v1; the passages used are identical).
- Retraction: none found. The Crossref DOI record has no relation or update fields, and `filter=updates` returns 0. The arXiv abs page (v1 only) has no withdrawal notice.
- Card:
  - Setting: two released checkpoints. The first is a Routing Transformer (RT) with 490M parameters and 22 layers, which accepts up to 8,192 tokens and reaches 33.2 test perplexity on PG-19. The second is a Local Transformer (LT) made from the same checkpoint by replacing the clustering heads with local attention: a 256-token window and a receptive field of about 5K tokens. The data are the PG-19 validation books (49 after one malformed book was removed), with 220K sampled target tokens. Ten targets per context are evaluated near the end of the input; the last 40 tokens are excluded because of an RT artifact. Scores are teacher-forced perplexity, plus two sequence-level tasks (Sec. 2.2, pp. 808–809). App. F repeats everything with a newly released 24-layer LT and finds the same trends (p. 820).
  - What it establishes:
    - Overall validation perplexity does not improve once the prefix grows beyond 2K tokens. RT plateaus after 2K, and LT flattens near 1K (Sec. 3, Fig. 1, pp. 809–810).
    - Small subsets do benefit, mainly for RT. Infrequent tokens (about 20K, 9% of targets) go from about 1200 to 1180 perplexity between 2K and 5K, then drift back to 1200. Later pieces of multi-piece words improve up to 5K. The 22K tokens last seen 2K–8K tokens back keep improving up to 8K. Fiction and continuous books benefit most (Sec. 3, Figs. 2–6, pp. 810–812). These subsets overlap little: overlap ratios are below 0.1 except for in-subword versus infrequent tokens (App. C, p. 819).
    - Perturbation was tested on RT only, because LT never used more than about 3K tokens (footnote 16, p. 812). Shuffling the distant prefix, or replacing it with text from another book, has minimal effect on overall perplexity as long as the nearest 2K tokens are intact; shuffled distant context even gives slightly lower perplexity (Fig. 7, p. 811; Sec. 4, p. 813). Subword-cluster tokens and copyable tokens are sensitive to both the order and the content of the distant context. Dropping the earlier occurrences of target tokens raises those targets' perplexity, so RT stores token identity to some extent (Figs. 10–12, pp. 812–813).
    - At the sequence level, context beyond 2K does not help suffix identification. Both models exploit a copied sequence only when it occurs within about 512 tokens (Sec. 5, Fig. 13, p. 814).
  - Headline numbers (verbatim, with location): "only improves their predictions on a small set of tokens" (Abstract, p. 807). Printed values: RT infrequent-token perplexity about 1200 at 2K and 1180 at 5K (p. 810). Subset sizes: 22K copyable-from-far tokens and 36K tokens absent from the prefix (p. 811).
  - Authors' own stated limitations or scope: only two models were analysed, because other long-range LMs such as the Compressive Transformer and Longformer had no public PG-19 checkpoints and differ in size (Sec. 2.2, p. 809). The RT end-of-sequence artifact was handled by excluding the last 40 tokens (footnote 1, pp. 808–809).
  - Relation to the review: supports RQ1 (importance). Distant context is used by a minority of target tokens (rare tokens, continuations of multi-piece words, tokens copyable from far back), and that use is shallow: it tracks token identity rather than order.
  - The hinge: the study measures teacher-forced perplexity on PG-19 books for two 490M-parameter models from 2021 with inputs of up to 8K tokens. The review asks about errors in answers generated by current LLMs.
- Use 1 — intended claim: "Giving long-range language models context beyond the most recent 2K tokens barely lowered overall perplexity; the small gains came from a minority of tokens, such as tokens that can be copied from the distant context, and perturbing the distant context barely mattered."
  - Source passage: "only improves their predictions on a small set of tokens" (Abstract, p. 807). The 2K plateau is in Sec. 3 (p. 810), and the perturbation result is in Sec. 4 (p. 813).
  - Scope: two models derived from one checkpoint; PG-19 only; perturbations tested on RT only.
  - Verdict: NARROW. "Long-range language models" here means two models from one checkpoint. The paper reports no discernible aggregate gain and identifies the subsets that improve, but it does not decompose an aggregate gain into those subsets. "Perturbing barely mattered" holds for overall perplexity only; the copyable and subword subsets were sensitive. Safe wording: "On PG-19 books, two long-range Transformer language models that accept up to 8K tokens (a 490M-parameter Routing Transformer and a local-attention variant of it) gained essentially nothing in overall perplexity from context more than 2K tokens back. Only small subsets of tokens benefited, mainly in the Routing Transformer: rare tokens, later pieces of multi-piece words, and tokens that could be copied from the distant context. Shuffling or replacing the distant context barely changed the Routing Transformer's overall perplexity as long as the nearest 2K tokens were intact [sun2021longrange]."
- Bib notes: taken from the ACL Anthology .bib (2021.emnlp-main.62). Authors, title, pages 807–822 and DOI agree with Crossref. Editors and month are omitted.

### kaplan2020scaling
- Source of record: arXiv:2001.08361 (2020), v1 only, with no later venue version. The arXiv record has no journal-ref; Semantic Scholar lists the venue as arXiv only. Access: full text (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.08361v1; p. 25 rendered at 600 dpi to read the Fig. 20 legend).
- Retraction: none found. Crossref has no record for this preprint (its DOI is registered with DataCite), and the title queries return no retraction notice. The arXiv abs page has no withdrawal notice.
- Card:
  - Setting: decoder-only Transformers trained on WebText2 with a 1024-token context (n_ctx = 1024 unless noted), scored by test cross-entropy (Sec. 2, p. 6). Fig. 20 (p. 25) has two panels. The left panel plots per-token test loss against position T for six model sizes. The right panel plots per-token loss against training step for a 774M-parameter model.
  - What it establishes:
    - For a fixed model size, per-token loss falls with position T. The fits printed in the Fig. 20 legend are 4.0+3.2·T^-0.47, 3.4+4.0·T^-0.56, 2.9+4.5·T^-0.56, 2.7+4.9·T^-0.60, 2.4+5.1·T^-0.61 and 2.3+5.4·T^-0.62 (p. 25). Larger models have larger exponents (Sec. 3.2.1, p. 9).
    - Models learn short-range information first and longer-range correlations later in training (App. D.5, p. 25). LSTMs match Transformers on early tokens but fall behind on later ones (Sec. 3.2.1 and Fig. 7, p. 9). Models trained with n_ctx = 8 beat the largest n_ctx = 1024 models on the very first tokens (D.5, p. 25).
  - Headline numbers (verbatim, with location): "Loss scales predictably as a power-law in T." (Fig. 20 caption, p. 25). Printed values: exponents 0.47–0.62 and additive constants 2.3–4.0 (Fig. 20 legend, p. 25).
  - Authors' own stated limitations or scope: the cause is left open, either power-law correlations in language or a more general feature of architecture and optimization (D.5, p. 25). No separate floor, plateau or saturation point is reported. The additive constant in each fit is its asymptote, and all fits cover T ≤ 1024.
  - Relation to the review: qualifies the uniform-risk assumption. Average teacher-forced loss differs systematically by position, falling as more context accumulates. This is background for RQ1/RQ2.
  - The hinge: the measure is average teacher-forced loss on held-out web text at each context position. The review asks about the per-decision error rate inside generated answers, and lower average loss late in the context does not by itself locate errors in generation.
- Use 1 — intended claim: "Per-token loss falls with a token's position in the context, roughly as a power law, before leveling off."
  - Source passage: "Loss scales predictably as a power-law in T." (Fig. 20 caption, p. 25). The same statement appears in the App. D.5 text (p. 25).
  - Scope: WebText2 test loss, 1024-token context, models trained for that context.
  - Verdict: NARROW. "Before leveling off" is not stated. The fits are a power law plus a constant, so the curve flattens toward a model-dependent constant within T ≤ 1024. Safe wording: "In Transformer language models trained on WebText2 with 1024-token contexts, test loss per token fell with the token's position T in the context, following fits of the form a + b·T^(−α) with α between 0.47 and 0.62 (larger for larger models), that is, a power-law decline toward a model-dependent constant [kaplan2020scaling]."
- Bib notes: @misc for an arXiv-only work. The ten authors, title and year come from the arXiv API record. howpublished, doi 10.48550/arXiv.2001.08361 and note = Preprint are set as the instructions require.

### olsson2022induction
- Source of record: https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/in-context-learning-and-induction-heads/index.html (Transformer Circuits Thread, Anthropic, published Mar 8, 2022). Also on arXiv as 2209.11895 (v1, Sept 2022), a PDF print of the article. Access: full text (the HTML article converted to text; the arXiv v1 PDF for page numbers, which are given as "arXiv p.").
- Retraction: none found. Crossref has no record (the article is not registered there), and the title query returns only other papers. The arXiv abs page has no withdrawal notice.
- Card:
  - Setting: 34 decoder-only Transformers from four series. There are small attention-only models (1–6 layers), small models with MLPs (1–6 layers), "full-scale" models (4 layers/13M parameters up to 40 layers/13B parameters) and "smeared-key" models. All have an 8,192-token context window and were trained on filtered Common Crawl, books and about 10% code (Model Analysis Table and Model Details, arXiv pp. 28–29).
  - What it establishes:
    - The article takes in-context learning to mean decreasing loss at increasing token indices. Its score is the loss difference between the 50th and 500th tokens (Key Concepts, arXiv p. 4). The article writes this difference in both orders (500th minus 50th in Key Concepts and the appendix, 50th minus 500th in Argument 1), but it reports the values as positive amounts of improvement.
    - The score develops abruptly in a narrow window early in training, roughly 2.5–5 billion tokens. It is under 0.15 nats before the window and roughly 0.4 nats after it, then stays constant and is similar across model sizes. The exception is one-layer models, where little in-context learning forms (Argument 1, arXiv p. 9). Model Details dates the small-model phase change at about 1–3 billion tokens.
    - Induction heads form abruptly in the same window, which also shows a bump in the training loss (Argument 1, arXiv p. 10). In small attention-only models, knocking out induction heads at test time greatly reduces in-context learning (Argument 3).
  - Headline numbers (verbatim, with location): "develops abruptly in a narrow window early in training" (Argument 1, arXiv p. 9). Printed values: under 0.15 nats before the window and roughly 0.4 nats after it; the window is 2.5e9–5e9 tokens (Argument 1).
  - Authors' own stated limitations or scope: the authors call the evidence preliminary and indirect. It is causal for small attention-only models and correlational for larger models with MLPs (Abstract; the Argument 1 evidence table rates the evidence for large models as medium-strength and correlational). Confounds and alternative hypotheses are possible, and a constant score does not imply constant mechanisms (Unexplained Curiosities).
  - Relation to the review: qualifies uniform per-position risk, since average loss is lower later in the context. It also supplies a copying-style mechanism (induction heads) for exploiting earlier context. This is a precedent for RQ1.
  - The hinge: the measure is the difference in averaged teacher-forced loss between two positions, tracked over training. The review asks where errors arise at inference in generated answers.
- Use 1 — intended claim: "Loss at late positions in the context is lower than at early ones, and this in-context improvement appears abruptly during training, together with induction heads."
  - Source passage: "develops abruptly in a narrow window early in training" (Argument 1, arXiv p. 9). That later tokens are easier to predict is stated in Key Concepts (arXiv p. 4). The co-occurrence with induction-head formation is in Argument 1 (arXiv p. 10) and the Abstract.
  - Scope: models trained by Anthropic, the token-50/token-500 measure, and a link that is correlational for larger models.
  - Verdict: NARROW. The improvement does not appear from nothing (under 0.15 nats existed before the window), and the induction-head link is correlational except in small attention-only models. Safe wording: "Language models' loss is lower at later positions in the context than at earlier ones. In the Transformer language models Olsson et al. studied (34 models, up to 13B parameters), most of this in-context improvement, measured as the loss difference between the 50th and 500th tokens, appeared abruptly in a narrow window early in training, the same window in which induction heads formed; outside small attention-only models, the authors present this link as correlational [olsson2022induction]."
- Bib notes: the author list, title, journal ("Transformer Circuits Thread") and year are verbatim from the article's "BibTeX Citation" block, and the author order matches the arXiv record. month = mar comes from the article's "Published Mar 8, 2022". The arXiv ID is given in a note.

### yang2025horizon
- Source of record: Transactions on Machine Learning Research, 2026. The TMLR accepted-papers list (https://jmlr.org/tmlr/papers/) gives "LLM Probability Concentration: How Alignment Shrinks the Generative Horizon", by Chenghao Yang, Sida Li and Ari Holtzman, September 2026. The TMLR BibTeX is at https://jmlr.org/tmlr/papers/bib/KotVuXj6CL.bib, and the OpenReview forum KotVuXj6CL was not opened because of its bot check. Access: full text (arXiv v4 https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.17871v4, dated 12 Sep 2026, whose header reads "Published in Transactions on Machine Learning Research (9/2026)"; v1 https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.17871v1 for the history).
- Existence and metadata: **the paper exists, but the assigned details are outdated.** v1 (22 Jun 2025) was titled "How Alignment Shrinks the Generative Horizon" and listed two authors, Yang and Holtzman. The published version adds the "LLM Probability Concentration:" prefix and Sida Li as second author. The venue is TMLR 2026, and no 2025 venue exists. v1 also claimed that alignment cuts the branching factor by nearly an order of magnitude. v4 narrows this to a factor of 2–5 overall, and up to about tenfold only at the first output positions.
- Retraction: none found. Crossref has no record and no retraction notice. The arXiv abs page lists v1–v4 and no withdrawal.
- Card:
  - Setting: Llama-2 and Llama-3 base and aligned models (8B/13B and 70B), sampled at temperature 1.0 with top-p 0.9, 50 samples per prompt. Tasks are MMLU, Cognac, BBCLatestNews, Creative StoryGen and a synthetic Random Strings task. For short outputs, BF comes from the realized entropy; for long outputs, length-averaged NLL is used as a proxy (Secs. 3–4, pp. 4–6). App. G extends the results to OLMo-2, Qwen, summarization and multilingual tasks (cited on p. 7).
  - What it establishes:
    - BF is the exponentiated length-averaged entropy of the output distribution. The authors read it as the effective number of plausible next-token choices per step (Sec. 3, Eq. 6, p. 4).
    - BF often drops smoothly as output tokens accumulate (Sec. 4.1, Fig. 3, p. 7). The authors stress this is an aggregate tendency, not a monotone token-wise law, and that local increases occur (pp. 7–8).
    - A prefix-substitution intervention attributes the decline mainly to autoregressive self-conditioning, which appears in base and aligned models alike. Unexpected injected content raises BF, which then decays again. Alignment sets the level and how fast BF falls back (Sec. 4.2, Fig. 5, p. 8).
    - Aligned models' BF is roughly 2–5 times lower than base models', and up to about an order of magnitude lower at the start of outputs (Sec. 4.1, p. 7).
  - Headline numbers (verbatim, with location): "BF often decreases as generation progresses" (Abstract, p. 1). Printed values: a reduction by a factor of 2–5 overall, and from 12 to 1.2 at the beginning positions (Abstract, p. 1).
  - Authors' own stated limitations or scope: they claim no monotone law and give no mechanistic account (Sec. 4.2, p. 8). The main experiments use mild decoding settings (footnote 1, p. 3). BF is estimated from samples, with NLL standing in for entropy on long outputs (Sec. 3, pp. 4–5).
  - Relation to the review: background for RQ2 (hardness). The number of plausible continuations is not constant across positions: it is typically larger early and smaller later, and it is smaller after alignment.
  - The hinge: BF measures the spread of the model's own output distribution, not correctness, so a low BF does not imply a correct token. The results come from Llama-2/3 models at temperature 1.0 and top-p 0.9 on the listed tasks.
- Use 1 — intended claim: "The effective number of plausible next tokens (a branching factor) falls as generation proceeds, and alignment tuning lowers it further."
  - Source passage: "BF often decreases as generation progresses" (Abstract, p. 1). The alignment effect is in the Abstract and in Sec. 4.1 (p. 7).
  - Scope: an aggregate tendency, not a monotone law; Llama-2 and Llama-3 families.
  - Verdict: NARROW. The decline is an aggregate tendency ("often"), and the authors tie it to self-conditioning rather than alignment. The title, the author list and the venue must also be corrected. Safe wording: "Yang, Li, and Holtzman measure a branching factor, the exponentiated length-averaged entropy of a model's output distribution, which they read as the effective number of plausible next tokens per step. In Llama-2 and Llama-3 base and aligned models sampled at temperature 1.0 with top-p 0.9, it often declined as generation proceeded, which a controlled intervention ties mainly to the model conditioning on its own output rather than to alignment; alignment tuning lowered it from the first tokens on, typically by a factor of 2 to 5 and by up to about ten at the earliest positions [yang2025horizon]."
- Bib notes: taken from the TMLR BibTeX export (title, three authors, journal, ISSN 2835-8856, year 2026, OpenReview URL), which matches the TMLR listing and the arXiv v4 byline. The key carries 2025 (the v1 year) while the publication year is 2026; the key was kept as assigned.

### hsieh2024ruler
- Source of record: COLM 2024 (First Conference on Language Modeling), OpenReview forum kIoBbc76Sy, as listed on https://colmweb.org/2024/AcceptedPapers.html. COLM hosts its papers on OpenReview, issues no DOIs, and has no page numbers. Access: full text (arXiv v3 https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.06654v3, dated 6 Aug 2024, with the header "Published as a conference paper at COLM 2024"; v1 for the history). The OpenReview camera-ready PDF was not opened because of a bot check.
- Retraction: none found. Crossref has no record (COLM issues no DOIs), and the title queries return no notice. The arXiv abs page lists v1–v3 and no withdrawal.
- Card:
  - Setting: 17 long-context models: 15 open models plus GPT-4 and Gemini-1.5-Pro, from 7B to 8x22B parameters, with claimed context lengths of 32K–1M. All were run with greedy decoding in vLLM, with 500 examples per task per length, at lengths of 4K–128K. Scores average 13 synthetic tasks. Eight are retrieval variants, ranging from passkey and vanilla needle-in-a-haystack to versions with distractor needles or multiple keys, values or queries. The rest are variable tracking (multi-hop), common- and frequent-word extraction (aggregation), and SQuAD and HotpotQA with distractor documents (App. B, Table 5, p. 18). A model "passes" a length if it exceeds the score of Llama2-7B (chat) at 4K, 85.6% (Sec. 4, p. 6).
  - What it establishes:
    - Passkey and vanilla NIAH scores are near perfect, but performance on the full suite falls sharply with length (Abstract; Sec. 4, p. 6).
    - The effective length is below the claimed length for every model except Gemini-1.5-Pro, which passes at every tested length up to 128K (Table 3, p. 7). Examples: GPT-4 reaches 64K of a claimed 128K, Yi-34B 32K of 200K, and LWM-7B less than 4K of 1M.
    - Counting the Table 3 entries above 85.6% at 32K gives 10 of 17 models, although the v3 abstract says only half the models pass at 32K. v1 (ten models) said only four models passed at 32K.
  - Headline numbers (verbatim, with location): "they fail to maintain performance above the Llama2-7B baseline at their claimed length" (Sec. 4, Main Results, p. 6; the sentence refers to every model except Gemini). Printed values from Table 3 (p. 7): threshold 85.6; GPT-4 claimed 128K and effective 64K; Gemini-1.5-Pro claimed 1M and effective >128K.
  - Authors' own stated limitations or scope (Sec. 8, p. 10): the benchmark does not control where the key information sits. The proxy tasks are not validated against realistic long-context tasks, so the authors present RULER as a behavioural check that should not be preferred over realistic settings. Short contexts are not evaluated, which should not be read as perfect ability at 4K; the authors cite FLenQA (levy2024same) here. Prompt robustness was only tested in preliminary experiments.
  - Relation to the review: concerns what extra input tokens cost, and qualifies the review. Performance on tasks beyond plain retrieval degrades as the input grows, well before the advertised limits.
  - The hinge: the study varies input context length on synthetic tasks with short outputs. The review asks about long generated outputs and about errors located at decisions.
- Use 1 — intended claim: "On synthetic tasks beyond simple retrieval, most long-context models performed well below their claimed context length."
  - Source passage: "they fail to maintain performance above the Llama2-7B baseline at their claimed length" (Sec. 4, p. 6). Supported by Table 3 (p. 7).
  - Scope: the 13-task average, a fixed qualitative threshold, and tests only up to 128K.
  - Verdict: NARROW. "Beyond simple retrieval" needs care, because the average includes eight retrieval variants; it goes beyond vanilla needle-in-a-haystack only.
