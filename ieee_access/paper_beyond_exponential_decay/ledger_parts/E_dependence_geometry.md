### huang2024cannot
- Source of record: https://openreview.net/forum?id=IkmD3fKBPQ (ICLR 2024, poster; OpenReview BibTeX booktitle "The Twelfth International Conference on Learning Representations"); Access: full text (https://arxiv.org/html/2310.01798, v2 of 14 Mar 2024, the version marked "ICLR 2024")
- Retraction: none found (Crossref bibliographic query, top 3: no matching record or notice; no Crossref notices update the arXiv DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2310.01798; arXiv abs page shows no withdrawal)
- Use 1 — intended claim: "intrinsic self-correction without external feedback fails and can degrade performance"
  - Source passage: "In the context of reasoning, our research indicates that LLMs struggle to self-correct their responses without external feedback, and at times, their performance even degrades after self-correction." — Abstract. "We observe that, after self-correction, the accuracies of all models drop across all benchmarks." — Sec. 3.2 (Tables 3-4). "For GSM8K, 74.7% of the time, GPT-3.5 retains its initial answer. Among the remaining instances, the model is more likely to modify a correct answer to an incorrect one than to revise an incorrect answer to a correct one." — Sec. 3.3, Fig. 1
  - Scope: prompted self-correction (review your answer, then revise), at most two rounds, no oracle labels. Models: GPT-3.5-Turbo-0613, GPT-4 (accessed Aug 2023), GPT-4-Turbo-1106-preview, Llama-2-70b-chat. Benchmarks: GSM8K, CommonSenseQA, HotpotQA (closed-book, 100 questions). The drop varies a lot: GPT-4 GSM8K 95.5 -> 89.0 after two rounds, Llama-2 GSM8K 62.0 -> 36.5, GPT-3.5 CommonSenseQA 75.8 -> 41.8 (Tables 3-4). The authors explain the failure as a judgment problem plus prompt bias ("The fundamental issue is that LLMs cannot properly judge the correctness of their reasoning." — Sec. 3.3). They give no internal-state or trajectory account. Limitation they state: reasoning only, and self-correction toward style or safety can work (Sec. 7).
  - Verdict: ACCURATE. Recommended wording that keeps the scope: "Without external feedback, prompting 2023-era LLMs (GPT-3.5/GPT-4 class, Llama-2-70B) to review and revise their own answers on reasoning benchmarks did not improve accuracy and often lowered it; correct answers were changed to incorrect ones more often than the reverse [huang2024cannot]." The persistence statistic (74.7% of answers unchanged) is theirs. Any "trajectory lock-in" reading is ours and must not be attributed to them.
- Other findings a reviewer would expect: (i) Gains reported by earlier self-correction papers came from oracle labels (Sec. 3) or weak initial prompts (Sec. 5). Multi-agent debate does no better than self-consistency with the same number of responses (Sec. 4, Table 7). (ii) The "Yet" in the title matters for a 2026 review. Later work trains models to self-correct, e.g. Kumar et al., "Training Language Models to Self-Correct via Reinforcement Learning" (ICLR 2025 oral; seen on OpenReview, not audited here), and long-CoT reasoning models revise within a trace. tsui2026blindspot (this batch) reports a small or even negative blind spot for reasoning models. (iii) The Kamoi et al. survey on when self-correction works (TACL 2024) is likely to be requested; not audited here.
- Bib: Huang, J.; Chen, X.; Mishra, S.; Zheng, H. S.; Yu, A. W.; Song, X.; Zhou, D. ICLR 2024, OpenReview IkmD3fKBPQ. ICLR has no pages or DOI (OpenReview-only proceedings).

### tsui2026blindspot
- Source of record: https://openreview.net/forum?id=rLscoLa2TM (COLM 2026; venueid colmweb.org/COLM/2026/Conference, i.e. accepted; OpenReview BibTeX booktitle "Third Conference on Language Modeling", 2026). The "COLM 2026?" venue is CONFIRMED, and the arXiv v3 comment reads "Accepted to COLM 2026". Earlier versions: a NeurIPS 2025 LLM Evaluation Workshop poster under the shorter title "...Revealing the Self-Correction Blind Spot in LLMs", and an ICLR 2026 submission (rejected). Cite the COLM version. Access: full text (https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02778, v3 of 2 Aug 2026, the COLM-accepted version)
- Retraction: none found (Crossref: no record or notices for 10.48550/arXiv.2507.02778; arXiv: no withdrawal)
- Use 1 — intended claim: "models fix an error when presented as external but not when it is their own (~64.5% blind spot)"
  - Source passage: "Testing 14 open-source non-reasoning models reveals a 64.5% Self-Correction Blind Spot: models correct external errors but fail on identical internal ones, proving the capability exists but is not activated." — Abstract. "Internal correction: the error is injected into the model's response via the assistant turn of the chat template, and the model is allowed to continue generating." — Sec. 3.1. "we therefore scope our claims to conversational role rather than authorship." — Sec. 4.2. "the magnitude is a property of the controlled design rather than an estimate of how often models fail to correct themselves in deployment." — Sec. 6
  - Scope: off-policy injected errors. The identical error text is placed in either the user turn or the assistant turn. Models: 14 open non-reasoning models, plus closed Claude 3.5 Haiku (52.5%) and Sonnet 4 (41.4%) (Table 7). Datasets: SCLI5 (286), GSM8K-SC (1,313), PRM800K-SC (448), and BIG-Bench Mistake logic/tracking (62.8% average, Table 8). Settings: temperature 0, 1,024-token budget, Gemini-2.5-Flash judge. The blind spot is defined as 1 - P(correct | internal) / P(correct | external), a relative measure (Eq. 1). On the models' own naturally generated (on-policy) errors, only "4.3% - 10.8%" were caught when re-presented externally (Sec. 4.1, Tables 9-10). Reasoning models show "a small, even negative" blind spot (Sec. 4.4).
  - Verdict: NARROW → safe wording: "When an identical error was injected into the model's own (assistant) turn rather than the user's turn, 14 open non-reasoning LLMs corrected it far less often — a 64.5% average 'self-correction blind spot' [tsui2026blindspot]. The author stresses that this magnitude reflects the controlled injected-error design; on models' naturally generated errors, only 4.3-10.8% were caught when re-presented as external." Why narrowed: in the paper, "their own" means the error is attributed to the model by conversational role, not that the model authored it. The 64.5% is not a deployment rate, and reasoning models largely lack the gap.
- Use 2 — intended claim: "appending 'Wait' reduces it substantially"
  - Source passage: "Appending "Wait" requires no training yet reduces the blind spot by 89.3%, and operates through a nearly independent pathway" — Abstract. "it reduces the blind spot by 89.3% on average (Figure 6) and increases mean accuracy by 156% (Figure 7)." — Sec. 4.1
  - Scope: the same non-reasoning models and datasets. "But" and "However" also work, but less well (Table 12). In Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen2.5-7B, "Wait" moves activations very little along the conversational-role direction (effective alpha -0.09 and -0.01; Sec. 4.2).
  - Verdict: ACCURATE (write "by 89.3% on average").
- Other findings a reviewer would expect: (i) Mechanism. The paper identifies a transferable "conversational-role direction": the mean internal-minus-external activation at the last error token. Steering along it at layer 13 causally increases correction (Sec. 4.2, Table 13). This is the authors' "direction in representation space" vocabulary; they use no manifold or trajectory language. (ii) LoRA fine-tuning on 5,306 error-correction traces reduces the blind spot by 76.0% (Sec. 4.3); the author traces the blind spot to SFT data that lacks correction sequences. (iii) The paper itself ties the blind spot to snowballing: "Zhang et al. (2024) demonstrate that once LLMs hallucinate, subsequent tokens often align with the initial error" (Sec. 2). Reviewers will expect that primary citation (Zhang, Press, Merrill, Liu, Smith, "How Language Model Hallucinations Can Snowball", ICML 2024; not audited here). (iv) An author interpretation that fits our framing, and is an interpretation rather than a measured propagation: "by correcting errors during generation rather than committing to them, reasoning models recover from mistakes that would otherwise propagate to incorrect final answers." (Sec. 4.4)
- Bib: Tsui, K. (single author). COLM 2026, "Third Conference on Language Modeling", OpenReview rLscoLa2TM.

### arora2022exposure
- Source of record: https://aclanthology.org/2022.findings-acl.58/ (Findings of ACL 2022, pp. 700-710, DOI 10.18653/v1/2022.findings-acl.58; ACL Anthology BibTeX); Access: full text. The published PDF (https://aclanthology.org/2022.findings-acl.58.pdf) is the basis for the quotes. arXiv v3 (https://arxiv.org/html/2204.01171, Jan 2023, a post-publication revision) was also read.
- Retraction: none found (Crossref record for the DOI: no updated-by/relation entries; the updates filter returns 0)
- Use 1 — intended claim: "exposure bias produces error accumulation in generation that perplexity does not capture"
  - Source passage: "We show that exposure bias leads to an accumulation of errors during generation, analyze why perplexity fails to capture this accumulation of errors, and empirically show that this accumulation results in poor generation quality." — Abstract (ACL version). "the length-normalized regret exhibit poor correlation with the entropy rate (ρ = 0.4003) indicating perplexity's failure to capture the error accumulation due to exposure bias." — Sec. 7.2, Fig. 2 (compare ρ = 0.9997 for per-step error)
  - Scope: GPT-2 117M; "We rely on GPT-2 model fine-tuned on Wikitext103 as our approximate oracle." (Sec. 6); 20k Wikitext-103 prompts of 50 tokens, completions up to 512 tokens. Here "error" means the per-step log-ratio loss log(o/p) between the approximate oracle and the model, accumulated as imitation-learning regret with bounds T*eps <= R <= T^2*eps borrowed from Ross and Bagnell (Sec. 4). It is not a task-level or factual error. Accumulated error grows "near-quadratically" with length (Sec. 7.1, Fig. 1a). Decoding matters: greedy and beam search accumulate more excess error than sampling (Table 1). The link to hallucination is a hypothesis, not a measurement. The mechanism is phrased as context drift: "This phenomenon reinforces itself as the context drifts further from what the oracle would produce, leading to an accumulation of errors." (Sec. 4). The paper does not use the word "trajectory".
  - Verdict: NARROW → safe wording: "Casting generation as imitation learning, Arora et al. found for GPT-2 (117M) completing Wikitext-103 prompts — scored against a fine-tuned GPT-2 used as an approximate oracle — that per-token divergence accumulates super-linearly with length, and that this accumulated regret correlates only weakly with perplexity (ρ ≈ 0.40, versus ρ ≈ 0.9997 for per-step error) [arora2022exposure]." Why narrowed: the claim is theirs, but the "errors" are divergences from an approximate oracle, measured in a small 2019-era model. Do not present this as evidence about wrong answers or decisions in current LLMs, and keep "trajectory" as our gloss on their "context drift".
- Other findings a reviewer would expect: (i) He et al. found self-recovery rather than incremental distortion ("Quantifying exposure bias...", arXiv 1905.10617, cited by Arora; later "Exposure Bias versus Self-Recovery...", EMNLP 2021). This is the standard counterpoint; not audited here. (ii) bachmann2024pitfalls (this batch) argues that the compounding-error and exposure-bias account presumes teacher forcing learned an accurate predictor. (iii) Fig. 1's caption says greedy decoding yields "over 70% more errors", while Table 1 lists 60.96% (greedy) and 69.72% (beam) %ExErrAcc. These are different aggregates, so quote the one that matches our sentence exactly.
- Bib: Arora, K.; El Asri, L.; Bahuleyan, H.; Cheung, J. C. K. Findings of ACL 2022, Dublin, pp. 700-710, DOI 10.18653/v1/2022.findings-acl.58. The ACL Anthology BibTeX abbreviates the fourth author to "Cheung, Jackie"; the paper byline reads "Jackie Chi Kit Cheung", which is used here.

### bachmann2024pitfalls
- Source of record: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/bachmann24a.html (ICML 2024, PMLR 235:2296-2318; PMLR BibTeX); Access: full text. The PMLR PDF (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v235/main/assets/bachmann24a/bachmann24a.pdf) is the basis for the quotes. arXiv v3 (https://arxiv.org/html/2403.06963, Jul 2025 revision) was also read.
- Retraction: none found
- Use 1 — intended claim (caution): "some failures come from teacher-forced training failing to learn the task, not from compounding errors at inference"
  - Source passage: "The popular criticism that errors can compound during autoregressive inference, crucially assumes that teacher-forcing has learned an accurate next-token predictor." and "in certain classes of tasks, teacher-forcing can simply fail to learn an accurate next-token predictor in the first place." — Abstract (PMLR PDF). "indeed, in our setting the error happens "instantaneously" at the beginning, rather than snowball over time." — Sec. 6
  - Scope: synthetic "path-star" graph path-finding (G_{d,l}; 200k training samples; node labels drawn from 100 values), tested in-distribution. Models: GPT-Mini trained from scratch, pretrained GPT-2 large (fine-tuned), Mamba trained from scratch. 3-digit addition appears in App. F.4. Test accuracy sits at about 1/d, the chance of guessing one of the d branches, despite perfect training fit (Sec. 5.1). Stated limitations: "We have also not demonstrated failure for very large models such as Llama2 ... or Mistral" and "It is also unclear if this failure generalizes to run-of-the-mill text-generation tasks." (App. A)
  - Verdict: ACCURATE. Recommended wording: "Bachmann and Nagarajan caution that the compounding-error argument presumes teacher forcing has learned an accurate next-token predictor; on a minimal synthetic path-finding task, teacher-forced Transformers and Mamba never learned the decisive first step, so failure occurred at the first token rather than accumulating along the sequence [bachmann2024pitfalls]."
- Other findings a reviewer would expect (all in a synthetic setting; their framing, not ours): (i) "Failure 1" formalizes snowballing as P(exact match) ≈ (1-ε)^L, the independent-per-token exponential model. They do not dispute it; they call it orthogonal to their failure (Sec. 3, App. B). (ii) Once the first node is fixed, the rest of the path follows it. The Clever-Hans test reports "Acc_cheat(LM) ≈ 100% almost across the board" (Sec. 5.1): the model continues whichever branch it is handed. This is consistent with later tokens cohering with a key decision, but the authors read it as a learned shortcut. App. F.1: with the first token teacher-forced, "the accuracy Acc_sb is barely affected even for graphs with very long paths L = 40", and "Failure 1 is not as prominent in this setting." (iii) App. H summarizes the stronger Kaariainen and Ross-Bagnell notion of snowballing: "once an erroneuous [sic] sub-optimal action is committed, the model is more likely to commit more sub-optimal actions". Cite that as their summary of prior work. (iv) Teacherless (multi-token) training rescues some of the easier graphs, and training on the reversed path makes the task learnable even from scratch (Sec. 5.1). (v) The abstract on the PMLR web page differs from the PDF abstract (the web page says "exponential error accumulation" is "a symptom of autoregressive inference"); quote the PDF.
- Bib: Bachmann, G.; Nagarajan, V. Proc. 41st ICML, PMLR vol. 235, pp. 2296-2318, 2024.

### marks2024geometry
- Source of record: https://openreview.net/forum?id=aajyHYjjsk (COLM 2024; OpenReview BibTeX booktitle "First Conference on Language Modeling"); Access: full text (https://arxiv.org/html/2310.06824, v3 of 19 Aug 2024, comment "Conference on Language Modeling, 2024")
- Retraction: none found
- Use 1 — intended claim: "at scale LLMs linearly represent truth/falsehood of factual statements"
  - Source passage: "Overall, we present evidence that at sufficient scale, LLMs linearly represent the truth or falsehood of factual statements." — Abstract
  - Scope: LLaMA-2-7B, 13B and 70B only ("we study only models in the LLaMA-2 family", Sec. 7.1). Data: curated, simple, unambiguous statements (cities, Spanish-English translations, numeric comparisons, and their negations, conjunctions and disjunctions) plus uncurated sets. The representations are localized to a few hidden states over the statement's final token and end-of-sentence punctuation (Sec. 3). Methods: PCA, probe transfer (LR, mass-mean, CCS) and interventions. In 7B, statements cluster by surface features (Fig. 3 caption). In 13B, some datasets separate along orthogonal or antipodal directions, and alignment improves with scale (Sec. 4). "Truth" is scoped as factuality, and the authors "cannot disambiguate truth from closely related features, such as "commonly believed" or "verifiable"" (Sec. 7.1).
  - Verdict: ACCURATE (keep "for simple factual statements" and "at sufficient scale").
- Use 2 — intended claim: "causal interventions can flip it"
  - Source passage: "Causal evidence obtained by surgically intervening in a LLM's forward pass, causing it to treat false statements as true and vice versa." — Abstract; Table 2 (normalized indirect effects)
  - Scope: the probe direction is added or subtracted at the group-(b) hidden states in a few-shot "This statement is: TRUE/FALSE" prompt on sp_en_trans statements, which are out-of-distribution for the probes. The effect size varies widely by probe, training set and model, from NIE 0.07-0.13 for some LR probes on 13B to about 1.0 for mass-mean probes (Table 2). What flips is the model's TRUE/FALSE judgment of a statement in its context, not the content it generates.
  - Verdict: ACCURATE, with wording: "shifting activations along the probe direction can make the model label false statements as true and vice versa, most effectively with difference-in-means (mass-mean) directions".
- Other findings a reviewer would expect: the authors speak of "linear structure" and "directions", never "regions" or "manifolds". If we describe truth as occupying a region or half-space, mark that as our gloss. Counter-evidence in this batch: orgad2025know finds truthfulness encoding "skill-specific" and generalization "not due to a universal internal encoding", and haller2025brittle finds separability degrades under surface perturbation. Likely to be requested but not audited here: Levinstein and Herrmann ("Still no lie detector...", probe failures on negation) and Burger et al. ("Truth is Universal", NeurIPS 2024, a 2-D truth subspace that generalizes across negation).
- Bib: Marks, S.; Tegmark, M. COLM 2024, OpenReview aajyHYjjsk. Title trap: the arXiv v3 PDF/HTML header shortens the title to "...in LLM Representations of True/False Datasets". The arXiv metadata and OpenReview both use "...in Large Language Model Representations of True/False Datasets"; use the latter.

### park2024linear
- Source of record: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/park24c.html (ICML 2024, PMLR 235:39643-39666; PMLR BibTeX; the arXiv journal-ref also states ICML 2024); Access: full text. The PMLR PDF (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mlresearch/v235/main/assets/park24c/park24c.pdf) is the basis for the quotes. arXiv v2 (https://arxiv.org/html/2311.03658) was also read.
- Retraction: none found
- Use 1 — intended claim: "formalizes linear representations of concepts with a causal inner product"
  - Source passage: "Using this causal inner product, we show how to unify all notions of linear representation." — Abstract. Definition 3.1: a causal inner product is an inner product with <γ_W, γ_Z>_C = 0 "for any pair of causally separable concepts W and Z" — Sec. 3.1
  - Scope: binary concepts defined by counterfactual word pairs; 27 concepts (22 from BATS, 4 language pairs, and frequent⇒infrequent). The representations are the final-layer context vector λ(x) and the token unembedding γ(y) of LLaMA-2-7B (Gemma-2B is compared in App. D.2). The causal inner product is estimated as Cov(γ)^-1 under an assumption about randomly drawn vocabulary words, and it is one member of a d-parameter family ("We do not have a principle for picking out a unique choice of D.", Sec. 3.2). The authors say: "we do not address interpretability of either model parameters, nor the activations of intermediate layers." (Sec. 5). One concept has no linear representation: "The sole exception is thing⇒part" (Sec. 4).
  - Verdict: ACCURATE. Recommended wording: "Park et al. formalize the linear representation hypothesis with counterfactual pairs and show that, under a 'causal inner product' estimated from the unembedding matrix, causally separable concepts are orthogonal and the probing and steering notions of linearity coincide, with supporting experiments on LLaMA-2-7B's final-layer representations [park2024linear]."
- Other findings a reviewer would expect: the results concern directions (1-D cones), not regions or manifolds, and are restricted to the final layer and to word-level binary concepts. Multi-dimensional, non-linear features are the known counterpoint (Engels et al., "Not All Language Model Features Are One-Dimensionally Linear", ICLR 2025; not audited here). The Golechha et al. ICLR 2025 blogpost (see park2025categorical) argues that orthogonality in the whitened space arises almost trivially in high dimensions.
- Bib: Park, K.; Choe, Y. J.; Veitch, V. Proc. 41st ICML, PMLR vol. 235, pp. 39643-39666, 2024.

### park2025categorical
- Source of record: https://openreview.net/forum?id=bVTM2QKYuA (ICLR 2025, oral; OpenReview BibTeX booktitle "The Thirteenth International Conference on Learning Representations"); Access: full text (https://arxiv.org/html/2406.01506, v3 of 18 Feb 2025)
- Retraction: none found
- Use 1 — intended claim: "categorical concepts form polytopes"
  - Source passage: "This allows us to immediately formalize the representation of categorical concepts as polytopes in the representation space. Further, we use the formalization to prove a relationship between the hierarchical structure of concepts and the geometry of their representations." — Abstract
  - Scope: a polytope here is the convex hull of the vector representations of a concept's values (Definition 7; for "natural" categorical concepts it is a simplex, App. C). It follows from the formalization and is illustrated empirically by projections in which tokens concentrate at the vertices (Fig. 6). Only the final-layer (softmax) geometry is covered: "we do not address the "internal" structure of the LLMs" (Sec. 2.1), and "this technique only works for the final layer representation" (Sec. 6). Models: Gemma-2B (main text) and LLaMA-3-8B (App. F). Concepts: WordNet, 593 noun and 364 verb synsets with at least 50 words in the vocabulary. Vectors are estimated with an LDA variant in the whitened unembedding space.
  - Verdict: ACCURATE (write "are represented as polytopes (simplices) in the final-layer representation space").
- Use 2 — intended claim: "hierarchy maps onto geometry — nested semantic regions"
  - Source passage: "hierarchically related concepts (such as plant⇒animal and mammal⇒bird) live in orthogonal subspaces, while categorical concepts are represented as polytopes." — Fig. 1 caption. "hierarchical concepts are represented as direct sums of polytopes." — Sec. 4.2 (Theorem 8: the child-minus-parent vector is orthogonal to the parent vector)
  - Scope: as in Use 1. Supporting evidence is in Figs. 4-5, with controls: a random-parent baseline, a shuffled-unembedding baseline, and a 70% token split that breaks set inclusion.
  - Verdict: NARROW → safe wording: "Park et al. show, theoretically and on Gemma-2B and LLaMA-3-8B using WordNet, that hierarchical relations are encoded as orthogonality — a child concept's vector is its parent's vector plus an orthogonal component — so hierarchical concepts form direct sums of polytopes [park2025categorical]." Why narrowed: "nested regions" is not the authors' geometry. They find an orthogonal decomposition, not containment. If we keep "nested regions", label it as our interpretation.
- Other findings a reviewer would expect: Golechha, Bushnaq, Ong, Kayal and Schoots, "Intricacies of Feature Geometry in Large Language Models" (ICLR 2025 Blogpost Track, 28 Apr 2025, https://iclr-blogposts.github.io/2025/blog/feature-geometry/), state that "their orthogonality and polytopes results are trivially true in high-dimensional spaces, and can be observed even in settings where they should not occur" (quoted from the blog's description; the full argument was read only in summary and not audited). A 2026 preprint attributes the same geometry to word co-occurrence (arXiv 2605.23821; seen in search results only, not read). The paper uses "orthogonal subspaces" and "polytopes", never "manifold".
- Bib: Park, K.; Choe, Y. J.; Jiang, Y.; Veitch, V. ICLR 2025, OpenReview bVTM2QKYuA.

### shai2024belief
- Source of record: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/8936fa1691764912d9519e1b5673ea66-Abstract-Conference.html (NeurIPS 2024; Advances in NeurIPS vol. 37, pp. 75012-75034; DOI 10.52202/079017-2387, which resolves in Crossref; proceedings BibTeX); Access: full text. The NeurIPS PDF (https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/8936fa1691764912d9519e1b5673ea66-Paper-Conference.pdf) is the basis for the quotes. arXiv v3 was also read.
- Retraction: none found (Crossref DOI record: no updated-by entries; the updates filter returns 0)
- Use 1 — intended claim: "residual stream linearly represents belief states over hidden data-generating states (even fractal geometry) — note the toy/synthetic setting"
  - Source passage: "we anticipate and then find that belief states are linearly represented in the residual stream of transformers, even in cases where the predicted belief state geometry has highly nontrivial fractal structure." — Abstract. "Our experimental validation focused on small-scale systems, using HMMs with only 3-5 states and vocabularies of 2-3 tokens." — Sec. 4.4
  - Scope: small transformers trained from scratch on sequences from stationary ergodic HMMs. Mess3 has 3 hidden states; RRXOR has 36 belief states in a 4-simplex. "Linearly represented" means an affine regression from residual activations onto the belief simplex; for Mess3 this is a 2-D subspace of the 64-d final-layer residual stream (Sec. 3.1). Controls: emergence over training, cross-validation, and shuffled labels (Fig. 6). For RRXOR, "the RRXOR belief state geometry is not represented in any of the individual layers of the transformer, but is represented in the concatenation of the layers." (Sec. 3.3). Not tested: a Bayesian-update circuit ("we do not yet know if these LLMs develop something like a circuit for implementing Bayesian updates", Sec. 4.4). For natural language, "the dimensionality of the belief state simplex would exceed the dimension of the residual stream" (Sec. 4.4), so any representation would have to be compressed; this is untested.
  - Verdict: NARROW → safe wording: "In small transformers trained from scratch on sequences from hidden Markov models with 3-5 hidden states and 2-3 tokens, Shai et al. found that an affine projection of residual-stream activations recovers the geometry of the optimal belief states over the hidden states, including a fractal geometry, in one case only when activations from all layers were concatenated [shai2024belief]. Whether LLMs trained on natural language carry a comparable (necessarily compressed) belief geometry is untested." Why narrowed: the title and abstract invite generalization to LLMs, but the evidence is entirely synthetic.
- Other findings a reviewer would expect: the authors' vocabulary is "belief state geometry", "simplex", "meta-dynamics of belief updating" and "synchronize", never "manifold" or "trajectory". Belief updating does trace paths through the simplex in their mixed-state presentation, so "trajectory" is defensible only as our gloss on it. Belief states carry information beyond the next token: for RRXOR, R^2 = 0.95 against belief-state distances versus 0.31 against next-token distances (Fig. 7D). Author-order trap: DBLP's NeurIPS record lists Shai, Teixeira, Gietelink Oldenziel, Marzen, Riechers, and the OpenReview NeurIPS BibTeX lists Shai, Riechers, Teixeira, Gietelink Oldenziel, Marzen. The proceedings PDF, proceedings BibTeX, Crossref and arXiv all give Shai, Marzen, Teixeira, Oldenziel, Riechers; use that order.
- Bib: Shai, A. S.; Marzen, S. E.; Teixeira, L.; Oldenziel, A. G.; Riechers, P. M. Advances in NeurIPS 37, pp. 75012-75034, 2024, DOI 10.52202/079017-2387.

### orgad2025know

- Use 2 — intended claim: "models can encode the correct answer internally yet generate a wrong one"
  - Source passage: "Lastly, we reveal a discrepancy between LLMs' internal encoding and external behavior: they may encode the correct answer, yet consistently generate an incorrect one." — Abstract.   - Verdict: ACCURATE, with wording: "a probe on the model's own activations can pick the correct answer out of its sampled candidates even when the model usually generates an incorrect one".
- Other findings a reviewer would expect: (i) Probe generalization across tasks rarely exceeds what logits alone achieve, and "the observed generalization is not due to a universal internal encoding of truthfulness" (Sec. 4). (ii) The error taxonomy over 30 samples includes "consistently incorrect" (the same wrong answer in at least half of the samples). That shows errors are systematic rather than independent draws, which helps the non-independence pillar, but it is across samples, not across positions. (iii) The paper is probe-based and uses no geometry or trajectory language. (iv) Follow-up work on "hidden knowledge" (Gekhman et al. 2025) is cited in v4; not audited here.

### haller2025brittle
- Source of record: https://openreview.net/forum?id=WcJN5D82ZV (COLM 2026; venueid colmweb.org/COLM/2026/Conference, i.e. accepted; OpenReview BibTeX booktitle "Third Conference on Language Modeling", 2026). This venue is NEW; the brief listed no venue. An earlier ICLR 2026 submission was rejected. Access: full text of arXiv v1 (https://arxiv.org/html/2510.11905, 13 Oct 2025, the only arXiv version). The COLM camera-ready was not opened (OpenReview bot challenge).
- Retraction: none found
- Use 1 — intended claim (counter): "truth representations collapse under typos/rephrasing — limits 'region = meaning'"
  - Source passage: "Our results reveal that internal representations of statement truthfulness collapse as the samples' presentations become less similar to those seen during pre-training." — Abstract. "While LLMs can often distinguish between true and false statements when they closely resemble the pre-training data, this ability is highly dependent on the statement's exact surface form." — Abstract
  - Scope: 10 models: OLMo 7B Base/Instruct, OLMo-2 Instruct 7B/13B, Llama 3.1/3.2 Instruct 1B/3B/8B/70B, and Gemma-3 4B/12B. Datasets: True-False (Azaria & Mitchell), MMLU, OpenBookQA, TruthfulQA. The abstract says "five evaluation datasets" but the Methods list four, so avoid the count. Probes (3-layer MLP, logistic regression, P(True)) are trained and tested on the transformed data. OOD-ness is proxied by perplexity, and robustness is the standardized slope of AUC against perplexity. Transformations: typos, punctuation noise, Yoda-style word order, French/Spanish translation, and negation. The decline is graded: for Llama-3.1-8B on True-False, β = -.43 (linear), -.46 (non-linear) and -.64 (P(True)) (Sec. 4); with the non-linear probe, Llama-3.1-70B is the least robust model (β = -1.53). General paraphrase was not tested: "reliably generating paraphrases that preserve meaning while ensuring sufficient distributional shift is non-trivial and difficult to automate." (Sec. 6)
  - Verdict: NARROW → safe wording: "Haller et al. found that the separability of true and false statements in LLM representations (linear and non-linear probes, and P(True)) degrades as statements are pushed out of distribution by typos, punctuation noise, unusual word order, or translation, across ten open models — a robustness limit on reading truth geometry as surface-invariant [haller2025brittle]." Why narrowed: meaning-preserving paraphrase was not tested and negation did no harm. "Collapse" is their abstract's word for a graded AUC decline. "Region = meaning" is our framing; theirs is "separability".
- Other findings a reviewer would expect: together with orgad2025know, this directly challenges probe-based "universal truth" readings (Marks & Tegmark). The authors separate brittle representations from brittle probes by training probes on the out-of-distribution data itself. On the MMLU subset the model answers correctly, AUC is higher but degrades at a nearly parallel slope (Sec. 5).
