### moshkov2025aimo
- Source of record: https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.16891 (NVIDIA technical report, single version v1 of 23 Apr 2025; arXiv comment "Report of AIMO-2 winning submission"). A shorter version, retitled "Scaling Mathematical Reasoning through Data, Tools, and Generative Selection", was a poster at the 2nd AI for Math Workshop @ ICML 2025 (https://openreview.net/forum?id=OM7ZN0PmSS). That workshop is non-archival, and every number we use comes from the arXiv report. Access: full text (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.16891v1; Table 7 read from https://arxiv.org/html/2504.16891).
- Retraction: none found. Crossref returned no matching record (arXiv DOIs are registered with DataCite), and the arXiv abs page shows no withdrawal.
- Use 1 — our sentence (Sec. 5 "Targeted compute at decision points", l. 125): "\citet{moshkov2025aimo}'s tool-integrated reasoning lets the model decide where to hand off to Python: it emits code blocks at the steps it judges need exact computation, under a budget of one to eight executions per solution, rather than computing everywhere."
  - Source passage: "for each problem we randomly select between 1 and 8 allowed code executions and provide this information in the prompt. ... As a result, model learns to follow specified code execution limit." (Sec. 3.4, p. 5). Also: "we want an ideal TIR solution to provide significant shortcuts by implementing otherwise infeasible brute-force approaches, e.g., using numeric solvers or conducting an exhaustive search of possible solutions." (Sec. 3.2, p. 4). Inference prompt: "You may perform up to {total_code_executions} Python code calls" (App. C, p. 35).
  - Scope: OpenMath-Nemotron 1.5B-32B (Qwen2.5-based) trained on 1.7M TIR solutions. The 1-8 range is the per-problem cap randomized while generating training data. At inference the cap is whatever the prompt states. The model does choose where to emit code. "Exact computation" is our gloss; the paper's criterion is novel, significant shortcuts.
  - Verdict: NARROW → suggested wording: "\citet{moshkov2025aimo}'s tool-integrated reasoning lets the model decide where to hand off to Python: it emits code blocks where it chooses---its training data favour code that buys a real shortcut, such as numeric solving or exhaustive search---under a code-execution cap stated in the prompt (randomized between one and eight per problem in training), rather than computing everywhere."
- Use 2 — our sentence (Sec. 5 "Strategic ensembles", l. 128): "\citet{wang2023self}'s self-consistency, \citet{yao2023tree}'s tree search, and \citet{moshkov2025aimo}'s GenSelect all exploit the convergence-of-correct-paths structure."
  - Source passage: "We observe a considerable gap in the majority@k vs pass@k performance for our models, implying the models theoretical ability to solve far more problems than can be achieved with a majority answer. To bridge this gap, we explore training a model that, given a set of candidate solution summaries, picks the most promising solution." (Sec. 4, p. 5)
  - Scope: GenSelect has the model reason over 2-16 candidate solution summaries and pick one. The final answer is a majority vote over 64 GenSelect runs.
  - Verdict: NARROW. GenSelect is motivated by exactly the cases where convergence (the majority) fails: it targets problems that pass@k solves but maj@k does not. Suggested wording: "\citet{wang2023self}'s self-consistency and \citet{yao2023tree}'s tree search exploit the convergence-of-correct-paths structure, and \citet{moshkov2025aimo}'s GenSelect targets the gap it leaves---problems a model sometimes solves but not by majority---by comparing sampled solutions and picking the most promising one."

  - Verdict: NARROW → suggested wording: "TextFooler \citep{jin2020textfooler}, catalogued with related attacks in \citet{morris2022textattack}, drives classifier accuracy below $15\%$ on every dataset except fake-news detection (below $10\%$ for most model--dataset pairs) by swapping $3.5\%$--$22\%$ of words depending on task and target model---$3.5\%$--$6\%$ on long IMDB reviews, about $14\%$--$19\%$ on short entailment pairs---and its ablation (BERT on MR, AG and SNLI) shows that choosing \emph{which} words to perturb is what does the work: with the importance ranking removed and words chosen at random, after-attack accuracy rises by more than $45$ points."
- Use 3 — our sentence (Sec. 6, l. 142): "the perturbation estimates range from $3.5\%$ to $22\%$ \citep{jin2020textfooler}."
  - Source passage: as in Use 1 (Tables 3-4, p. 5).
  - Scope: the paper's number is not an estimate of k/n. It is the share of words a greedy, importance-ranked synonym attack changes before a classifier's label flips. That makes it an upper bound on the minimal change, measured on classification inputs rather than generated text.
  - Verdict: NARROW → suggested wording: "an importance-ranked synonym attack must change $3.5\%$ to $22\%$ of words to flip a text classifier \citep{jin2020textfooler}."
